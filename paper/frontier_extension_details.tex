\subsection{Separating Support from Rank}\label{app:frontier_factorial}

The dense SNR result changes the first-map family as well as the second-map
rank. We therefore cross sparse versus dense first-map updates, both with
rank ceiling $16$, with second-map ranks $8$ and $12$. The original SNR
development references, seeds $800$--$802$, retain their data splits, Adam
moments, $25$ continuation epochs, dose $1$, and future masked fitting
orders. Both calibrations use the same $1024$ fitting inputs and the same
second-map right factors. Detached and joint arms are paired within every
cell. No test metrics are computed.

At second-map rank $8$, dense minus sparse joint accuracy is $+0.37$ points

\subsection{Opening a Complete Nonlinear Suffix}\label{app:nonlinear_suffix}

All three pretrained backbones use a fixed healthy reference and split:
$42{,}952$ CIFAR-100 fitting inputs, $5000$ validation inputs and $2048$
isolated anchors. A fixed Bernoulli mask provides $8705$ fine-labeled
fitting inputs. Healthy fine-$100$ and coarse-$20$ affine heads are fitted
on that same subset. Coarse labels are derivable from fine labels; they
are not an inaccessible-annotation assumption. Continuation does not use
coarse labels or a consumer-preservation loss.

The ImageNet-V1 ResNet-18 and ImageNet-V2 ResNet-50 use $64\times64$
bilinear input resizing; ImageNet-V1 ViT-B/32 uses $224\times224$. The
ImageNet input normalizer is fixed. Official torchvision weight URLs and
SHA-256 fingerprints are retained. The pretrained parameter count describes
the source backbone, separately from the subset updated during transfer.

The final-map screen tests unrestricted and rank-$16/64/128$ updates,
head-only continuation F, a width-$128$ residual GELU head N, and a linear
probe H on healthy features concatenated with pooled pre-projection
features. None of its three-epoch pilots reaches the two-point gain over F.
Refitting primary readers with the original standardized LBFGS/L2 recipe
recovers much of the online-head decline, without clearing that gate on
any backbone. These negative screens remain separate from the following
ten-epoch recipe.

The complete-suffix study opens both convolutions of ResNet-18's final
identity block, all three maps of ResNet-50's final identity block, or both
maps of ViT's final CLS MLP. Normalization parameters stay fixed. For CNNs,
the suffix BN transforms are folded before defining its two versions;
their deviation is measured against the pre-existing reference arithmetic.
Earlier computation is frozen and may be cached during training. Inference
evaluates the actual prefix.

Training uses ten AdamW epochs, weight decay $10^{-4}$, current-head rate
$10^{-4}$ and suffix/factor rate $3\times10^{-5}$. CE includes $10^{-4}$
times squared primary-head weights and bias; N includes its trainable
linear layers in that penalty. This explicit head regularization differs
from AdamW decay. Conditioning uses the two-view healthy fitting mean and
SD, floored at $0.1$ times median positive SD. The initial affine head is
rebased to retain its function in real arithmetic. The old readout is unchanged.

Views are clean input and one fixed CIFAR training augmentation per labeled
image: reflection padding $4$, a uniform $32\times32$ crop, and horizontal
flip with probability $1/2$, using RNG $3901$ in fitting order. All learned
arms use the same original fitting-index orders (seed$+20000$) and view
choices (seed$+71000$). Final primary readers refit the same two views and
labels, with no encoder changes. Online heads and final refits are retained
as distinct objects. The final-map-only L control uses the same recipe;
R64 permits rank-$64$ changes to every suffix map.

Development requires at least two accuracy points over healthy and F,
within half a point of U, N and H, and at most $1\%$ disagreement for each
old class/selective consumer. ResNet-18 U passes all three seeds
$3800$--$3802$; the choice is publicly frozen before test loading. Ten
fresh continuation seeds $3810$--$3819$ then gain
$\SuffixRNGainHead$ points $[\SuffixRNGainHeadLow,\SuffixRNGainHeadHigh]$
over F and $\SuffixRNGainRichHead$ $[\SuffixRNGainRichHeadLow,\SuffixRNGainRichHeadHigh]$
over H (Table~\ref{tab:suffix_confirmation}). U exceeds L by
$\SuffixRNGainLastMap$ points. These intervals concern continuation on
one fixed healthy reference, label mask and data split, not variation across
independently pretrained models or populations.

\subsection{How Much of the Transformer Must Learn?}\label{app:attention_scope}

The complete final-MLP pilot does not reach the utility gate on ViT-B/32.
Opening its complete final attention block raises validation accuracy to
$75.64\%$, against $73.64\%$ healthy, $74.70\%$ F and $75.06\%$ H. The
$0.94$-point gain over F still fails. Neither screen evaluates official
test data. A final fixed scope check opens the last two blocks, freezing
the first ten and retaining every LayerNorm parameter. The first suffix
block processes all tokens; the last needs only its CLS query and MLP.
Independent full-transformer output and input-gradient checks verify this
pruning, including dynamic-batch TorchScript evaluation.

Under the same views, rates, loss and final-refit recipe, U reaches
$77.04\%$, $77.00\%$ and $76.96\%$ on development seeds $3800$--$3802$.
All three clear the fixed gate; M, which opens only the final MLP maps,
remains near $75\%$. This choice is publicly fixed before official test
loading. The scope comparison tests nonlinear learning capacity under one
recipe. Prefix sharing, late fine-tuning and low-rank updates are established
operations, rather than new learning algorithms.

\subsection{Matched CPU Implementations}\label{app:frontier_native}

For the small MLPs, a fused float32 C++ kernel uses the same transposed
weight layout, SIMD/compiler flags and preallocated buffers for protected
and current-only execution. Empty binding cost and C-internal repetition
timing are retained separately. Development chooses the fastest absolute
implementation: native at batch $1$, TorchScript at $1024$. This choice is
frozen before independent full-corpus serving checks on the ten saved HAR
and dense SNR states. The original TorchScript and slower native large-batch
paths remain in the evidence. A better ratio against a slower baseline is
not treated as a deployment improvement.

For vision, monolithic, modular-prefix and grouped dense suffix programs
compete against equivalently optimized one-version current programs.
Grouped convolutions and batched projections apply to both one- and
two-version implementations. The packed program does not win by assumption.
FP32 ResNet-18 development serving is about $1.12\times$ at batch $1$ and
$1.20\times$ at $128$; ViT is about $1.12/1.112\times$. The full-precision
batch-$128$ results miss the $1.10$ target. H reuses a healthy trace for its
own richer features and old head, so it has a different, cheaper frontier.

Timings use an isolated twelve-thread CPU process, twelve warmups and
$31$ alternating repetitions. Current-only, protected and independent
function evaluations receive the same preprocessing and pruning. Input
decoding/preprocessing, compilation and warmup are outside forward latency.
The chosen ABI is fixed on development, not selected on test timing. Full
chosen-code replay is separated from isolated timing; identical H functions
are counted once, with each seed's monitor certificate recomputed from its
actual saved output arrays. Untimed frozen-prefix reuse checks reference,
source, corpus and state fingerprints.

Two export jobs fail before timing because a reference-only comparator
registered the same readout tensor twice. A subsequent completed ResNet-18
record is retained despite a queue-envelope bookkeeping error; only the
pending ViT job is reissued. These technical events, exact executed source
versions and logs are retained. They do not change training or selection.

\subsection{Precision Belongs to the Consumer Contract}\label{app:reference_precision}

The protected-only precision probe keeps the current function and shared
prefix at FP32. Reference matrix products use BF16; their results return to
FP32 before adding the original biases. Norms, attention, activations,
residuals and the frozen affine consumer remain FP32. This changes the
reference implementation, so its claim is a measured decision contract,
not the exact-function identity of the FP32 construction.

On $5000$ development inputs, ResNet-18 class/80/50 disagreements are
$0.32/0.28/0.18\%$; ViT disagreements are $0.02/0.06/0.08\%$. Both pass
the same one-percent screen, using only paired outputs for precision
selection. No labels, fitted temperatures or output corrections select the
precision. Maximum logit errors are $0.104$ and $0.087$, respectively;
the class and selective decisions are checked directly.

Standard whole-encoder CPU AMP, retaining the FP32 affine consumer, is a
stronger numerical control. It changes ResNet-18 class/80/50 decisions on
$1.28/1.72/0.98\%$ of inputs, missing the screen even though accuracy rises
from $54.00\%$ to $54.10\%$ and aggregate coverage remains close. ViT's
$0.20/0.50/0.26\%$ passes the decision screen and proceeds to the fixed
three-state utility control. Its matched lower-precision current-only
baseline is reported separately from a fixed-current-FP32 ratio.

These numerical operations are standard. Low-bit language-model studies also
show decision-dependent margin changes~\citep{wu2026decisions}. Here the
precision choice tests a named legacy interface alongside retained current
utility and measured CPU cost. A small aggregate metric change supplies no
replacement for the class and fixed-threshold audits.
