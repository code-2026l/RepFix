\title{Silent Scale Shift: When Shared Updates Break Existing Readouts}
\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Shared encoders can keep learning while breaking the readouts that depend on them. We study this failure under auxiliary-task updates and identify a scale-sensitive component, \emph{silent scale shift}: task information remains decodable, but an existing readout loses accuracy. In matched HAR continuations across ten seeds, frozen-readout accuracy falls from $\CpuHarHealthy$ to $\CpuHarJointMid$, while the current head reaches $\CpuHarOwnMid$; unlabeled scale correction restores $\MechHarScalarMid$. With a real reconstruction auxiliary, error falls to $\ReconHarMseRatioHigh$ of detachment while frozen accuracy drops to $\ReconHarFrozenHigh$; paired linear alignment restores $\ReconHarPairedLinearHigh$. Exact affine boundaries explain the scale mechanism, and a repair hierarchy separates it from richer drift. A signed update audit shows why task-gradient alignment and unsigned variance probes can miss these changes. Finally, an independently confirmed SNR experiment retains a $\BenefitGuardGain$-percentage-point auxiliary gain and the old logits under a reference-anchored constraint, at $\BenefitLatencyRatio\times$ CPU inference cost; folding that constraint into the encoder removes the cost but loses the gain. A coordinate map fitted after unrestricted learning avoids both penalties, keeping a $\ChartGain$-point gain at $\ChartLatency\times$ native cost and improving old-reader accuracy, yet it changes $\ChartDisagreement\%$ of legacy decisions and no repaired consumer meets a five-point disagreement budget. Readout compatibility is therefore a separate acceptance criterion, not a corollary of accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

An encoder and its downstream readouts need not update together. Auxiliary training changes the features; a deployed classifier may retain the weights and input statistics fitted to an earlier version. The current task head can adapt to this change and remain accurate. What happens to the readout already in use?

We isolate a large compatibility failure by cloning a healthy HAR model and its Adam state, retaining the fitted readout, and continuing matched branches on identical minibatches. Across ten seeds at variance-auxiliary dose $3$, frozen-readout accuracy falls from $\CpuHarHealthy$ to $\CpuHarJointMid$, although the jointly trained head reaches $\CpuHarOwnMid$. A single global input-scale correction, fitted without new labels, restores $\MechHarScalarMid$ (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b). We call this scale-sensitive component \emph{silent scale shift}. The information needed for the task remains available; the existing readout has become incompatible with its input.

This failure also occurs with a real auxiliary objective. HAR reconstruction lowers held-out MSE to $\ReconHarMseRatioHigh$ of the dose-matched detached decoder, while frozen-readout accuracy falls to $\ReconHarFrozenHigh$. A linear map fitted to paired old and new training features restores $\ReconHarPairedLinearHigh$, leaving the encoder and reconstruction head unchanged (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). None of the five seeds meets the scatter-collapse criterion. Auxiliary progress and readout failure can therefore coexist before that criterion signals a problem.

We study the failure through three complementary tests. First, exact affine-readout boundaries and a fixed repair hierarchy identify the contribution of scale. Second, a signed chain-rule audit explains what task-gradient relations and an unsigned radial probe leave unresolved. Methods such as PCGrad~\citep{yu2020pcgrad} and CAGrad~\citep{liu2021cagrad} act on task-gradient relations; variance change also depends on the update's alignment with the variance gradient. For Adam, we additionally measure the applied parameter increment. Third, we test whether useful auxiliary learning can coexist with an unchanged readout.

For the third test, a reference-anchored readout-nullspace control~\citep{shen2020backward,farajtabar2020ogd} preserves healthy logits while leaving $53$ of $64$ feature directions free. Ten new seeds confirm a $\BenefitGuardGain$-point auxiliary gain at $\BenefitLatencyRatio\times$ CPU inference cost. Freezing the nonlinear trunk removes that cost but loses the gain. A post-hoc single-encoder chart retains the gain and improves old-reader accuracy, yet changes $\ChartDisagreement\%$ of old predictions. Decodability, aggregate utility, and decision compatibility require separate measurements.

\section{When Existing Readouts Fail}\label{sec:formulation}

Let $h_\theta(x)\in\mathbb R^m$ be the shared features. An existing readout applies the fixed map $W_0D_0^{-1}(h_\theta(x)-\mu_0)+b_0$, with healthy means $\mu_0$, positive diagonal scales $D_0$, weights $W_0$, and bias $b_0$. We use \emph{consumer} for this complete input-to-output map. During continuation, the current task head learns with the encoder, while the old consumer stays fixed. A refitted probe tests what the new features can support; the old consumer tests whether its interface still works.

Pure contraction already separates these two questions. For $H_c=cH$, an affine readout has logits $cWh+b$, which approach $b$ as $c\to0$. A unique bias winner then makes the prediction constant, although every $c>0$ preserves linear decodability. The following boundaries locate the relevant class changes.

\begin{proposition}[Two scale boundaries of a frozen affine readout]\label{prop:scale_law}
Write the logits after fixed input statistics as $z_i(c)=z_0+c\,s_i$. Assume unique winners $t=\arg\max_jz_0[j]$ and $p_i=\arg\max_jz_i(1)[j]$. The bias-only class first loses as $c$ increases from zero at
\begin{equation}
 c_i^{\rm bias}=\min_{j:\,s_i[j]>s_i[t]}\frac{z_0[t]-z_0[j]}{s_i[j]-s_i[t]},
 \label{eq:critical_scale}
\end{equation}
with the minimum of an empty set taken as $\infty$. The first class change while shrinking from $c=1$ instead occurs at
\begin{equation}
 c_i^{\rm first}=\max_{\substack{j:\,s_i[p_i]>s_i[j]\\0<\xi_{ij}<1}}\xi_{ij},\qquad
 \xi_{ij}=\frac{z_0[j]-z_0[p_i]}{s_i[p_i]-s_i[j]},
 \label{eq:shrink_first}
\end{equation}
with zero denoting no positive-scale flip. These boundaries can differ in multiclass problems. With nonzero feature variances and exact statistics, refitting the input statistics makes the readout invariant to every $c>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each pairwise margin is affine in $c$. Increasing $c$ from zero reaches the smallest positive crossing of the bias winner; decreasing $c$ from one reaches the largest crossing of the healthy winner in $(0,1)$. Standardising $cH$ cancels $c$ from numerator and denominator. For example, logits $(1,10c,-1+12c)$ give $c^{\rm bias}=0.1$ but $c^{\rm first}=0.5$.
\end{proof}

The imposed-scale sweep in Figure~\ref{fig:scale_law} tests the scale mechanism (Appendix~\ref{app:readout}). It isolates scaling from the additional drift learned during training.

The matched HAR trajectory in Figure~\ref{fig:paired_dose}a starts from one healthy checkpoint and excludes calibration examples from encoder and readout fitting. At $\alpha=3$, the current head recovers while the frozen consumer remains inaccurate. Repairs fitted to training features without new labels distinguish mean and scale changes: mean correction reaches $\MechHarMeanMid$, global scale correction $\MechHarScalarMid$, and their combination $\MechHarMeanScalarMid$, close to per-feature recalibration ($\CpuHarRecalMid$). At $\alpha=100$, per-feature repair reaches $\CpuHarRecalHigh$ versus $\CpuHarRetrainHigh$ after refitting. Scale explains much of the moderate-dose failure; stronger or projected contraction requires richer alignment (Appendix~\ref{app:repair_hierarchy}).


Repairs operate upstream of the frozen weights, through a versioned encoder output or consumer-input adapter. Marginal repairs need cached healthy statistics and unlabeled new features; paired alignment also needs old features of the same anchors. A supervised refit can reuse existing training labels and is a valid, stronger-access baseline. We report NLL and $15$-bin ECE alongside accuracy.

\section{Auditing Shared Updates}\label{sec:theory}\label{sec:diagnosis}

\subsection{Signed variance change}

Let $H(\theta)\in\mathbb R^{B\times m}$ be the outputs of a shared encoder on a fixed diagnostic batch; a linear primary head and an auxiliary head consume $H$. Training minimizes $L_p+\alpha L_a$, where $L_p$ is the primary loss, $L_a$ the auxiliary loss, and $\alpha\ge0$ the dose; multiple auxiliaries can be absorbed into their weighted sum. Write $G_p=\nabla_\theta L_p$ and $G_a=\nabla_\theta L_a$. Define
\begin{equation}
 V(\theta)=\frac{1}{2B}\|C_BH(\theta)\|_F^2,
 \qquad C_B=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/B,
 \qquad v=\nabla_\theta V.
 \label{eq:mtl_obj}
\end{equation}
Here $2V$ is the covariance trace with divisor $B$: a rotation-invariant measure of variation across examples. Under gradient flow,
\begin{equation}
 \dot V=r-\alpha c,\qquad r=-v^\top G_p,\qquad c=v^\top G_a.
 \label{eq:signed_variance}
\end{equation}
We call $c>0$ \emph{auxiliary contraction}, and $r>0$ \emph{primary restoration}. When both are positive, $\alpha c/r>1$ defines the instantaneous contraction-dominated regime. Reporting $(r,c)$ also covers a primary update that itself reduces variance. This identity retains the encoder Jacobian; it defines a signed diagnostic without a reduced dynamical model.

\begin{proposition}[Conflict information does not identify contraction]\label{prop:conflict_gap}
The task-gradient Gram matrix does not determine the sign of $\dot V$. In particular, there are two auxiliary updates with identical task-gradient norms and conflict, but opposite effects on variance under the same primary update.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $G_p=e_1$, $G_a^\pm=\pm e_2$, and $v=-e_1+\beta e_2$ for $\beta>0$. Both pairs have identity Gram matrix and zero conflict, whereas $r=1$ and $c^\pm=\pm\beta$. For $\alpha\beta>1$, $\dot V^+<0<\dot V^-$. These gradients are locally realizable by a smooth encoder and losses (Appendix~\ref{app:boundary_proof}).
\end{proof}

Task-gradient relations therefore leave variance change unidentified. A combiner may preserve variance by reducing the auxiliary dose, but preserving a particular auxiliary update requires checking its projection onto $v$. The fixed-dose comparisons below test this distinction.

The local balance also differs from eventual collapse. In the symmetric linear model $\dot z=(R-\alpha A)z$, $R\succ0$, $A\succeq0$, some mode contracts when $\alpha\lambda_{\max}(R^{-1/2}AR^{-1/2})>1$; all modes contract when $\alpha\lambda_{\min}(R^{-1/2}AR^{-1/2})>1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}). A head can exploit the surviving modes. In HAR, even a $4{,}600\times$ trace contraction leaves standardized-probe accuracy unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:scale_audit}). We therefore measure variance, effective rank, task decodability, and old-readout utility separately.

\subsection{What an unsigned probe leaves out}

Let $h$ and $u$ stack the rows of $H$ and $C_BH$, respectively, and let $g_p=\nabla_h L_p$, $g_a=\nabla_h L_a$. A cheap feature-space probe uses the centered radial direction:
\begin{definition}[Unsigned radial toxicity proxy]\label{def:toxicity}
For $u\ne0$ and $\|g_p\|>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \rho_b=\frac{\|\Pi_u g_a\|}{\|g_p\|},\qquad
 \Pi_u g=\frac{u^\top g}{\|u\|^2}u.
 \label{eq:order}
\end{equation}
The experimental estimator $\hat\rho$ instead averages regularized per-example ratios. The frozen decision uses a recorded value $q=\hat\rho_{t_0}$ and the nominal dose $\alpha_{\rm probe}=1/q$.
\end{definition}

The proxy discards the sign of the auxiliary direction and uses the entire primary-gradient norm as its reference. Both choices matter: expansion and contraction can give the same $\rho_b$, and a large primary gradient need not restore centered variation. There is also a measurement mismatch. The controlled driver probes the isotropic surrogate $\|C_BH\|_F^2/(Bm)$ but trains with fixed projected-variance auxiliaries; the real-head driver probes the actual auxiliary. The signed audit below accounts for these choices.

\subsection{A local validity boundary}

The discrepancy can be exposed without assuming radial training dynamics. Let $J=\partial h/\partial\theta$, $K=JJ^\top$, $e=u/\|u\|$, and $n_p=\|g_p\|>0$. At an audited checkpoint choose any $k>0$ and write $K=k(I+E)$. Define
\begin{equation}
 a=\frac{e^\top g_a}{n_p},\quad d=-\frac{e^\top g_p}{n_p},\quad
 \epsilon_a=\frac{e^\top E g_a}{n_p},\quad
 \epsilon_p=-\frac{e^\top E g_p}{n_p}.
 \label{eq:boundary_terms}
\end{equation}
Here $a$ is signed radial loading relative to the primary norm, $d$ measures restoration alignment, and the $\epsilon$ terms measure the effect of encoder geometry. For the actual auxiliary, $|a|=\rho_b$. Products with $K$ can be evaluated by Jacobian--vector and vector--Jacobian products, without constructing the full matrix.

\begin{theorem}[Local validity boundary of a frozen probe]\label{thm:probe_boundary}
For any frozen probe value $q\ge0$, the variance dynamics satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot V}{N}=1-\alpha q+\mathcal E,\qquad
 N=\frac{k\|u\|n_p}{B}>0,\qquad
 \mathcal E=(d-1)+\epsilon_p-\alpha(a-q+\epsilon_a).
 \label{eq:probe_discrepancy}
\end{equation}
Consequently, with
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=|d-1|+|\epsilon_p|+\alpha\bigl(|a-q|+|\epsilon_a|\bigr),
 \qquad |1-\alpha q|>\Delta,
 \label{eq:validity_margin}
\end{equation}
the probe and the exact variance derivative agree in sign. If $1-\alpha q>\Delta$, variance increases; if $1-\alpha q<-\Delta$, it decreases. The remaining region requires the signed audit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The chain rule gives $\dot V=-u^\top K(g_p+\alpha g_a)/B$. Substitute $K=k(I+E)$, divide by $N$, and add and subtract $1-\alpha q$. The triangle inequality bounds $|\mathcal E|$ by $\Delta$.
\end{proof}

The margin is sufficient and checkpoint-local. Computing it requires the exact flux, so it serves as an offline explanation of the cheap probe. On $405$ HAR checkpoints, the frozen certificate fires on $2$; the signed balance $\psi=d-\alpha a$ with a combined residual certifies $10$ at the same scale. Per-checkpoint scale optimization raises the counts to $15$ and $154$ (Table~\ref{tab:probe_arms}). Every certified sign is correct. The corrected balance alone agrees in sign on $58\%$ of checkpoints, leaving substantial encoder-geometry mismatch.

In the identity metric ($E=0$), $\dot V/N=d-\alpha a$. If $a=q$ and $0<d<\alpha q<1$, the nominal gate stays closed while variance contracts: the primary norm overstates restoration. If $a=-q$, the same unsigned gate can open while the auxiliary expands variance. Drift after calibration contributes through $a-q$. These examples identify concrete sources of a wrong sign.

The mismatch term also separates into actionable components. If $a_0$ is signed loading at calibration for the actual auxiliary, then
\begin{equation}
 |a_t-q|\le |a_t-a_0|+|a_0-|a_0||+\bigl||a_0|-q\bigr|.
 \label{eq:mismatch_split}
\end{equation}
The terms separate temporal drift, discarded sign, and surrogate or aggregation mismatch. A radial approximation is informative when the auxiliary contracts, the primary gradient restores centered variation, the encoder metric is compatible with the approximation, and calibration transfers to the audited checkpoint.

A scalar reduction, $\dot\sigma=-\alpha\kappa\sigma+b(\sigma)$, yields a saddle-node threshold $\alpha_c=\lambda_\star/\kappa$ under the explicit shape and endpoint assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:transition}. Equating $1/q$ with this threshold additionally requires calibration to $\kappa/\lambda_\star$. Empirically, we compare $1/q$ with the half-height crossing $\alpha_{1/2}$ and report the operational collapse threshold separately.

\section{Evidence Across Tasks and Protocols}\label{sec:experiments}\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Protocols and observables}\label{sec:unified_protocol}

The controlled benchmark spans HAR~\citep{ucihar}, RadioML~\citep{radioml}, battery health~\citep{nasa_battery}, and NYUv2. A three-layer MLP with a fixed projected-variance auxiliary isolates contraction; Transformer, CNN, real-head, CelebA, and financial studies test its scope. Appendices~\ref{app:tables}--\ref{app:moved} give the protocols and optimizer grids. Error bars show population seed SD unless stated otherwise.

The operational label is relative scatter $s=\mathcal S_h/\mathcal S_{\rm single}<0.3$, using the same-seed single-task reference and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_h=\mathbb E_b\!\left[\operatorname{std}_j\!\left(h_j^{(b)}-\mathbb E_{b'}h_j^{(b')}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:cross_sample_scatter}
\end{equation}
This coordinate-dependent proxy misses variation along the all-ones feature direction. We use ``collapse'' for the declared $0.3$ criterion and measure information separately. The log-dose interpolated crossing at $s=0.5$, $\alpha_{1/2}$, summarizes the dose curve.

\subsection{Calibration is task- and width-dependent}\label{sec:validation}\label{sec:har}\label{sec:radioml}\label{sec:battery}\label{sec:nyu}\label{sec:capacity_law}

Across sixteen domain--width cells, the ratio of predicted dose to observed half-height crossing is $0.95$--$3.01$ for battery and $30$--$116$ for RadioML; HAR reverses bias with width (Figure~\ref{fig:probe_prediction}a). All cells train projected auxiliaries and probe the isotropic surrogate. The calibration error varies substantially across domains even in this controlled setting.

\subsection{Calibration and drift change the decision}\label{sec:negatives}

On HAR at $m=64$, detached warm-up moves $1/\overline{\hat\rho}$ from $1.34$ at $W=1$ to $0.00907$ at $W=4$, against a half-height crossing of $0.00141$. At dose $0.03$, $W=1,2$ leave all gates closed and all ten seeds collapse; $W\ge4$ opens every gate and prevents collapse. At dose $100$, all tested warm-ups protect scatter (Figure~\ref{fig:probe_prediction}b,d). An extreme dose can therefore hide a calibration error.

Recomputing the probe during training can produce false alarms. HAR ratios grow even at zero auxiliary dose, and running gates activate in every seed of ten configurations with final scatter $0.50$--$1.04$. Battery has one activation among 35 seeds ending above half-height (Appendix~\ref{app:online}). A shrinking primary-gradient denominator can inflate the ratio. On $405$ HAR checkpoints, the unsigned predictor agrees with the exact variance derivative on $51\%$, with median restoration alignment $0.065$.

\subsection{Real auxiliaries and stronger encoders}\label{sec:real_aux}

Four real auxiliary heads further separate loading from contraction (Figure~\ref{fig:conflict_toxicity}a). Mean per-example task-gradient cosines have magnitude at most $0.06$, while SNR radial loading is $88\%$. Yet SNR's endpoint signed radial cosine is negative, and $c_t=v^\top G_a<0$ at $74/75$ historical epoch-end checkpoints. Its relative collapse cannot be attributed to persistent positive sampled flow contraction. Flow and actual Adam signs also disagree on $\CpuRmlAuditDisagree/\CpuRmlAuditN$ sampled paired updates. We record both the applied increment and a primary-only Adam counterfactual (Appendix~\ref{app:cpu_paired}).

Protocol changes alter the observed failure. Historical SNR scatter onset lies between doses $3$ and $10$, whereas I/Q reconstruction stays above the criterion through $10000$. Under healthy-checkpoint continuation, I/Q at $3000$ gives frozen accuracy $\ReconRmlFrozenHigh$ versus $\ReconRmlPrimary$ detached, with trace $\ReconRmlTraceHigh$; SNR at $30$ gives $\CpuRmlJointThirty$ versus $\CpuRmlPrimary$, with scatter $\CpuRmlScatterThirty$. Resetting auxiliary weights or their Adam state also fails to reproduce the historical severe collapse (Appendix~\ref{app:head_state}). Warm-up, split, architecture, and horizon define the operating regime.

A waveform CNN on the same I/Q split reaches $\CnnHealthy$ healthy-readout accuracy. At reconstruction dose $3000$, frozen accuracy is $\CnnFrozenHigh$ versus $\CnnPrimary$ detached, while the current head reaches $\CnnOwnHigh$. This stronger encoder shows little consumer damage on the fixed real-auxiliary grid (Appendix~\ref{app:cnn_continuation}).

\section{Repairing and Preserving Compatibility}\label{sec:stf}\label{sec:stfcal}

Real reconstruction tests repair while the auxiliary makes progress (Table~\ref{tab:reconstruction_full}, Appendix~\ref{app:reconstruction_paired}). At HAR dose $100$, frozen accuracy falls from $\ReconHarPrimary$ under detachment to $\ReconHarFrozenHigh$, while the current head reaches $\ReconHarOwnHigh$. Test reconstruction MSE falls to $\ReconHarMseRatioHigh$ of detachment, with $0/5$ scatter collapses. Per-feature recalibration reaches $\ReconHarRecalHigh$; paired linear alignment reaches $\ReconHarPairedLinearHigh$ (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). Repair changes only the consumer input, retaining the auxiliary improvement.

Let $A=W_0\operatorname{diag}(1/s_0)$ and let $P_\perp$ project onto $\ker A$. The reference-anchored control $H_0+(H_\theta-H_0)P_\perp$ preserves all healthy logits while emitting one shared $64$-vector (Proposition~\ref{prop:consumer_nullspace}, Figure~\ref{fig:nullspace_control}). Validation selects SNR dose $1$ from six fixed candidates, using $20\%$ of fitting primary labels. Ten new seeds confirm ordinary auxiliary gain J--D of $\BenefitJointGain$ percentage points, paired $95\%$ interval $[\BenefitJointGainLow,\BenefitJointGainHigh]$, and guarded gain GJ--GD of $\BenefitGuardGain$, $[\BenefitGuardGainLow,\BenefitGuardGainHigh]$ (Table~\ref{tab:benefit_confirmation}). The guarded model meets the declared $0.5$-point noninferiority margin against J. All guarded predictions, NLL, and ECE match the reference; maximum logit error is $\BenefitLogitError$. The ten guarded joint endpoints also pass a separate five-point disagreement budget. Ordinary frozen accuracy barely changes, but ECE rises from $\BenefitHealthyECE$ to $\BenefitJointECE$. Both encoders remain at inference, at a measured $\BenefitLatencyRatio\times$ CPU cost (Appendix~\ref{app:benefit_confirmation}).

With a frozen nonlinear trunk, the last-affine constraint folds into one encoder but loses the SNR gain; freezing accounts for $-3.53$ points, while the constraint adds $-0.003$ (Appendix~\ref{app:compiled_interface}). A nonlinear-side grid also fails its benefit screen. Fitting and folding a chart \emph{after} unrestricted learning instead retains a $\ChartGain$-point gain at $\ChartLatency\times$ native CPU cost. Old-reader accuracy improves by $\ChartOldGain$ points and its NLL and ECE fall, yet $\ChartDisagreement\%$ of its predictions change, and none of ten repaired consumers meets the fixed five-point disagreement budget (Appendix~\ref{app:posthoc_chart}). Accuracy, calibration, current-head preservation, and cost can all pass while the deployed interface still changes.

Detachment blocks auxiliary gradients to the encoder and provides the reference when the auxiliary brings no primary-task value. The spectral toxicity filter (\STF{}) instead removes a chosen component of the auxiliary channel. Its idealized backward form is
\begin{equation}
 F_w[g_a]=g_a-w\Pi_u g_a,\qquad w\in[0,1],
 \label{eq:stf}
\end{equation}
The implemented selective arms transform the auxiliary input, $\widetilde H=H-wC_BHP$, using a frozen projector $P$ or a centered radial direction. The transformation changes the forward input as well as its gradient, while the auxiliary head continues training (Appendix~\ref{app:general}).

The calibrated arm freezes the warm-up probe and opens the gate when $\alpha\hat\rho\ge1$. The rank variant then selects
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{rank}_{\rm sel}=\min\{\mathrm{rank}\le \mathrm{rank}_{\max}:\alpha\hat\rho_{\rm res}[\mathrm{rank}]<1\},
 \label{eq:calrule}
\end{equation}
from nested candidate subspaces. If none passes, it uses the cap and records the unresolved residual. The radial arm is a separate intervention. Theorem~\ref{thm:minimalrank} gives the minimum deletion rank for a specified PSD operator; using an empirical PCA basis adds a basis-identification question. Gate calibration and subspace choice therefore need separate evaluation.

\label{sec:conflict_ablations}
The low-label SNR dose scan shows both the value and limits of filtering (Figure~\ref{fig:intervention}a,b). With $20\%$ primary labels, joint training is healthy at low dose but collapses in every seed from $\alpha=30$. Calibrated STF avoids collapse at every sampled dose and exceeds detachment by $0.5$--$1.0$ percentage points over $\alpha\in[15,100]$; the advantage disappears at extreme doses. This five-seed scan uses a different label budget from the fully labeled study in Appendix~\ref{app:rml_dose}.

The fully labeled SNR comparison shows why clearing the scatter threshold is insufficient (Figure~\ref{fig:intervention}c). Detachment reaches $0.307$ accuracy with $0/10$ collapses. The radial arm reaches $0.199$ with $1/10$, and the rank-3 arm $0.211$ with $0/10$. Basis choice also changes how much auxiliary learning is removed: in HAR reconstruction, an auxiliary-gradient basis uses $30.75$ directions versus PCA's $62.0$ among the same four gate-open seeds, at similar accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:realbasis}).

\label{sec:bmtl}\label{sec:bmtl_ablation}\label{sec:finance}
At the controlled high doses, calibrated filtering reduces collapse from $10/10$ to $0/10$ on HAR, battery, and RadioML, and from $5/5$ to $0/5$ on CelebA Smiling~\citep{liu2015celeba}. With a Transformer at RadioML dose $3000$, joint training collapses in $10/10$ seeds at accuracy $0.156$; filtering gives $0/10$ collapses and relative scatter $0.970$. Reliable gate activation at these extreme doses coexists with the calibration errors in Figure~\ref{fig:probe_prediction}.


\section{Evaluating a Shared Representation}\label{sec:standard}

The evidence suggests three questions for evaluation: how did the features change, what task information remains decodable, and does the existing consumer still work? Algorithm~\ref{alg:survival_audit} combines these checks with the signed update audit.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Auditing representation survival and probe validity}\label{alg:survival_audit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Diagnostic batch, healthy reference, checkpoint $\theta$, operating losses and dose $\alpha$, recorded calibration value $q$.
\STATE Report task metrics, covariance trace $2V$, effective rank, standardized linear probe, and the declared operational scatter criterion.
\STATE Audit healthy-readout accuracy/NLL/ECE, mean/global/per-feature repairs, and any paired alignment; compare with a refitted probe.
\STATE With unlabeled monitor inputs and healthy reference predictions, bound consumer disagreement; select a certified candidate or abstain.
\STATE Report gradient-flow fluxes $(r,c)$ from \eqref{eq:signed_variance}. For Adam, audit $v^\top s_\theta$ and the actual post-step $V$ change; specify any auxiliary counterfactual.
\STATE Record $1-\alpha q$, the signed balance $\psi=d-\alpha a$, and the corresponding residuals. Form $q^\star=a/d$ only when $d\ne0$.
\IF{$|1-\alpha q|>\Delta$}
\STATE Mark the gradient-flow sign locally certified for this batch.
\ELSE
\STATE Use the signed audit; report restoration misalignment, geometry, and probe mismatch separately.
\ENDIF
\STATE Compare joint training, detachment, and any selective intervention using both survival and task utility at matched doses and seeds.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When $\Delta$ exceeds the probe margin, the unsigned decision remains unresolved. The signed audit attributes its current error to restoration alignment, encoder geometry, discarded sign, or calibration mismatch. Repeating the same unsigned ratio cannot separate these effects.

The intervention comparison also needs both compatibility and task utility. A rank-3 SNR filter can pass the scatter criterion while underperforming detachment; the low-label radial filter can lose its advantage at extreme doses. An open gate alone says little about the quality of the selected subspace.

For a present checkpoint, unlabeled inputs can support a conservative consumer decision. On a fixed corpus, the increase in classification error is bounded by disagreement with healthy predictions. We sample $1{,}024$ monitor inputs, select the first candidate in a fixed repair hierarchy whose disagreement bound meets the budget, and otherwise abstain. Across $\ConsumerJointN$ joint endpoints, a $10$-percentage-point budget certifies $\ConsumerSelectedTen$, with $\ConsumerFalseSafeTen$ observed violations and $\ConsumerAbstainTen$ abstentions; a healthy-reference scatter heuristic makes $\ConsumerScatterFalseSafeTen$ false-safe declarations (Appendix~\ref{app:consumer_audit}).

The checks have different scopes. The signed flux diagnoses the current gradient-flow direction; Adam needs its applied increment. The STF gate is an empirical calibration rule. The disagreement bound covers the fixed checkpoint family and unmonitored corpus remainder, with access to healthy predictions; it leaves changed populations and future updates open. The CNN and single-encoder controls likewise limit the evidence for broadly useful, inexpensive protection.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Multi-task learning~\citep{caruana1997multitask} and task grouping~\citep{standley2020tasks} ask when shared training helps. PCGrad and CAGrad address gradient conflict~\citep{yu2020pcgrad,liu2021cagrad}; MGDA, Nash-MTL, and FAMO optimize Pareto, bargaining, or progress-balancing criteria~\citep{sener2018multi,navon2022multi,liu2023famo}. Other methods adapt task-gradient geometry or loss weights~\citep{senushkin2023aligned,liu2021imtl,chen2018gradnorm,kendall2018multi,wang2021gradient,limarenko2026gcond,zhou2025imgrad}. We add the variance gradient to this picture: task-gradient relations alone leave the sign of variance change unidentified.

Backward-compatible learning studies model-version compatibility~\citep{shen2020backward,hu2022backward}. LDC~\citep{gomezvilla2024ldc} already shows retained discrimination behind drifting class prototypes and learns label-agnostic feature maps. Our question concerns fixed affine consumers under auxiliary updates, separating scale, useful learning, and preserved decisions. CORAL~\citep{sun2016coral}, AdaBN~\citep{li2016adabn}, and Tent~\citep{wang2021tent} establish alignment and unlabeled adaptation controls; our anchor-fitted repairs evaluate model drift without adapting on test inputs. Shift and model-selection assumptions matter~\citep{zhao2023pitfalls}. Calibration~\citep{guo2017calibration,ovadia2019trust}, probes~\citep{alain2016probe}, and unlabeled performance estimation~\citep{garg2022unlabeled} complement the fixed-corpus disagreement check.

Self-supervised learning studies collapse through stop-gradient mechanisms~\citep{grill2020bootstrap,chen2021simsiam,caron2021emerging}, variance and covariance regularization~\citep{zbontar2021barlow,bardes2022vicreg,ermolov2021whitening}, and spectral dynamics~\citep{jing2022understanding,dong2021attention,kim2025taxonomy}. RankMe evaluates representations by effective rank~\citep{garrido2023rankme}. Our observable is variation across examples, distinct from terminal classifier geometry in neural collapse~\citep{papyan2020prevalence,wang2026neuralcollapse}. Input repair shows how reduced variation can coexist with retained decodability and lost readout compatibility.

Auxiliary-gradient projection~\citep{dery2021attittud,zhu2025gradops}, auxiliary selection~\citep{navon2021auxiliary,jiang2023forkmerge,shin2024dgr}, weight-space nullspace adaptation~\citep{pham2026ness}, and mergeable frozen-backbone updates~\citep{hu2022lora} provide the context for our intervention controls. Activity recognition~\citep{ucihar}, modulation recognition~\citep{radioml}, and battery health~\citep{nasa_battery,severson2019data,attia2020closed,pradhan2022soh} provide the test domains. We use these controls to measure compatibility, useful adaptation, and cost.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

An accurate current head can conceal a broken existing readout. Matched continuation and fixed input repairs expose this gap, with exact affine boundaries explaining its scale-sensitive component. Signed audits identify what gradient alignment and unsigned probes miss. The SNR confirmation shows that auxiliary benefit and unchanged old logits can coexist, while the single-encoder controls expose the cost of preserving both. Shared-representation evaluation should therefore measure what new features can predict and whether existing readouts can still use them.

\section{Proofs and Audit Details for the Diagnostic Boundary}\label{app:boundary_proof}


Let $\theta\in\mathbb R^2$, $v_0=-e_1+\beta e_2$, and $z(\theta)=v_0+\theta$.
Choose a two-example encoder $H(\theta)=(z^\top,-z^\top)^\top$.
Then $V=\|z\|^2/2$ and $\nabla_\theta V=v_0$ at $\theta=0$.
The losses $L_p=\|z-(v_0-e_1)\|^2/2$ and
$L_a^\pm=\|z-(v_0\mp e_2)\|^2/2$ are nonnegative smooth functions
of the representation. Their gradients at zero are $e_1$ and $\pm e_2$.
Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:conflict_gap} concerns realizable encoder/loss
configurations, not arbitrary incompatible gradients. Both task pairs have
the same Gram matrix, while the signed variance derivatives differ.

Vectorization of the centered batch gives $u=(C_B\otimes I_m)h$ and
$\nabla_\theta V=J^\top u/B$. Hence
$\dot V=-u^\top JJ^\top(g_p+\alpha g_a)/B$.
For any fixed positive scale $k$, write $JJ^\top=k(I+E)$.
This is a decomposition, not an assumption of isotropy.
With the definitions in \eqref{eq:boundary_terms}, the normalized derivative is
$d+\epsilon_p-\alpha(a+\epsilon_a)$, which proves
\eqref{eq:probe_discrepancy}. Taking absolute values term by term proves
\eqref{eq:validity_margin}. The exact discrepancy $\mathcal E$ can be smaller
than $\Delta$ because signed terms cancel. Failure of the sufficient margin
condition therefore leaves the probe undecided; it does not prove the probe wrong.
Choosing $k$ must be reported, since it changes the decomposition and the tightness
of this conservative envelope, although it cannot change $\dot V$.

Record the batch, checkpoint, losses, auxiliary dose, probe value $q$, and scale $k$.
Compute $g_p$, $g_a$ for the actual operating losses and $u$ from the unfiltered
representation. Evaluate $K g$ by first computing $J^\top g$, then a Jacobian--vector
product with $J$; a dense $K$ is unnecessary. This yields $a,d,\epsilon_a,\epsilon_p$,
the exact signed flux, and the validity margin. When $u=0$ or $\|g_p\|=0$, the
normalized decomposition is undefined and the unnormalized identity
\eqref{eq:signed_variance} remains the appropriate diagnostic.
This audit costs more than a frozen feature-space probe: at $B=256$ and $m=64$ on
CPU the exact flux costs $0.30$\,s while the exact $k=\operatorname{tr}(K)/(Bm)$
certificate costs $20.7$\,s, a factor of $69.6$
(\texttt{results/CERTCOST/}). The trace enters only through the canonical scale:
an envelope at any other $k$, including the per-checkpoint minimizer of
Table~\ref{tab:probe_arms}, needs the exact flux and the head-level gradients but
no trace of $K$. The certificate is therefore an offline audit, not a
cheap substitute for measuring the flux; the cheap object in the deployed rule is
the probe $q$. Its purpose is to identify why the cheap decision can or cannot be
transported to an operating checkpoint.

\begin{corollary}[Transport to a later audited checkpoint]\label{cor:transport}
Let $q_0$ be the probe frozen at calibration, and let $q_t$ and $\Delta_t$ be the
live probe and the envelope of Theorem~\ref{thm:probe_boundary} at a later
checkpoint. If
\[
|1-\alpha q_0| > \Delta_t + \alpha|q_t-q_0|,
\]
then the frozen decision has the same sign as the exact variance derivative at
that checkpoint.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$|(1-\alpha q_0)-(1-\alpha q_t)|=\alpha|q_t-q_0|$. If the displayed inequality
holds, then $|1-\alpha q_t|\ge |1-\alpha q_0|-\alpha|q_t-q_0|>\Delta_t$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:probe_boundary} applies at $t$ with the live terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Signed directional balance]\label{cor:corrected}
Let $\psi=d-\alpha a$. With the decomposition of Theorem~\ref{thm:probe_boundary},
\[
\frac{\dot V}{N}=\psi+\epsilon_p-\alpha\epsilon_a .
\]
If $|\psi|>|\epsilon_p-\alpha\epsilon_a|$ at a specified $k>0$, then $\dot V$ has the sign of $\psi$, including when $d=0$. When $d\ne0$, define $q^\star=a/d$ and $\Delta^\star=|\epsilon_p-\alpha\epsilon_a|/|d|$; the same condition is $|1-\alpha q^\star|>\Delta^\star$, with predicted sign $\operatorname{sign}[d(1-\alpha q^\star)]$. The $|d-1|$ discrepancy is absent.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Regroup \eqref{eq:probe_discrepancy} as $d-\alpha a+\epsilon_p-\alpha\epsilon_a$. A perturbation smaller in magnitude than $\psi$ cannot change its sign. Substitution of $q^\star$ gives the ratio form when $d\ne0$.
\end{proof}

The quantities $a\propto\sum_i(h_i-\bar h)^\top g_{a,i}$ and $d\propto-\sum_i(h_i-\bar h)^\top g_{p,i}$ are the centered auxiliary and primary radial derivatives. Their signed ratio $q^\star$ allows cancellation across examples. The archived probe instead averages per-example magnitudes divided by per-example primary norms. Replacing that primary reference by $d\,n_p$ removes the $|d-1|$ discrepancy.

Corollary~\ref{cor:transport} requires the frozen margin to exceed the current envelope plus probe drift. It certifies $2/405$ checkpoints at the canonical trace-normalized scale and $14/405$ under scale optimization, with no incorrect firing. The corresponding frozen certificate without the added drift term fires on $15/405$. Outside the certified checkpoints, calibration remains an empirical decision rule.

Let $s_\theta$ be the actual proposed parameter increment, including any
optimizer preconditioning, momentum, and weight decay. If $\nabla V$ is
$L$-Lipschitz on the segment from $\theta$ to $\theta+s_\theta$, then
\begin{equation}
 \left|V(\theta+s_\theta)-V(\theta)-v^\top s_\theta\right|
 \le \tfrac L2\|s_\theta\|^2.
 \label{eq:finite_step_audit}
\end{equation}
This follows by integrating the gradient along the segment. A first-order
margin exceeding this remainder certifies the sign of the finite-step change.
The gradient-flow decomposition applies directly to plain gradient updates;
the actual-step audit is the corresponding observable for Adam. Separating
primary and auxiliary contributions through a nonlinear optimizer requires
specifying the counterfactual update and its state. A feature-gradient norm
ratio is not an Adam-step certificate.

\subsection{Conditional Dynamical Models}

\begin{definition}[Degenerate minimizer]\label{def:degmin}
A loss has a degenerate minimizer if a constant representation attains its minimum. For a differentiable interior minimum, stationarity is necessary but insufficient. Variance shrinkage has such minima; reconstruction with varying targets generally does not, because a constant code need not be stationary for a fixed decoder. Accordingly, we evaluate trained reconstruction heads empirically.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional scalar collapse transition]\label{thm:transition}
Assume an independently justified scalar reduction
\begin{equation}
\dot\sigma=-\alpha\kappa\sigma+b(\sigma),\qquad\kappa>0,
\label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
where $b(0)=0$. Let $f(\sigma)=b(\sigma)/\sigma$ on $(0,\infty)$ tend to zero at both ends, increase strictly to a unique nondegenerate maximum $\lambda_\star=f(\sigma_\star)>0$, and decrease strictly afterwards. Then $\alpha_c=\lambda_\star/\kappa$. For $0<\alpha<\alpha_c$ there are two positive equilibria, the smaller unstable and the larger stable; initial values below the smaller one still collapse. At $\alpha_c$ the equilibria coalesce in a saddle-node; for $\alpha>\alpha_c$, every positive trajectory tends to zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vector field is $\sigma(f(\sigma)-\alpha\kappa)$. The crossings and their stability follow from monotonicity; the nonzero second derivative gives the nondegenerate fold. Above the maximum the field is strictly negative for every $\sigma>0$, whose only possible limiting equilibrium is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Departure from a primary optimum]\label{lem:irreversible}
At $G_p=0$, any nonzero $G_a$ makes the joint gradient nonzero when $\alpha>0$. This implies departure from the primary optimum, but neither collapse nor irreversibility.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-dose attenuation]\label{cor:stf}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:transition}, a constant auxiliary multiplier $q\in(0,1]$ replaces the effective dose by $q\alpha$ and the threshold by $\alpha_c/q$. Positive attenuation can therefore preserve the positive equilibrium at a fixed dose. Exact deletion removes the contraction term but is not necessary for fixed-dose protection.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[First modal contraction versus full linear collapse]\label{thm:spectral}
For the linear symmetric model $\dot z=(R-\alpha A)z$, assume $R\succ0$ and $A\succeq0$ in the same coordinates. Along an eigenvector of $R-\alpha A$ with eigenvalue $\nu_j$, the modal variance obeys
\begin{equation}
\dot\varsigma_j^2=2\nu_j\varsigma_j^2.
\label{eq:modal}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
T=R^{-1/2}AR^{-1/2},\qquad \rho_\star=\lambda_{\max}(T).
\label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
Some mode contracts iff $\alpha\rho_\star>1$. Every mode contracts exponentially iff $\alpha\lambda_{\min}(T)>1$. Equality leaves a neutral mode.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$R-\alpha A=R^{1/2}(I-\alpha T)R^{1/2}$ has the same inertia as $I-\alpha T$ by congruence. Since the generator is symmetric, its eigenvalue signs determine modal growth or decay.
\end{proof}


The scalar model and the symmetric linear model answer different questions.
Neither follows from measuring the proxy $\hat\rho$ alone. For example, with
$R=I$, $A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)$, and $\alpha=1$, one mode contracts and
another expands. This permits task performance on a surviving direction
without preserving the full representation. The NYUv2 result is compatible
with this mechanism but does not identify its dynamical eigenvectors.

\section{Financial Ranking Application}\label{app:finance}

The motivating observation came from multi-task equity selection. A production model shared an encoder of A-share factors across pairwise ranking, distributional (MDN~\citep{bishop1994mixture}), reconstruction (AE), and view-contrastive (SCARF-style~\citep{bahri2022scarf}) heads. After deployment, predicted rankings became nearly constant under the unchanged training objective: cross-sectional prediction spreads fell by approximately $93\%$ relative to validation while individual training losses stayed low. The observation prompted us to measure feature and prediction variation alongside loss; identifying its cause required the controlled studies.

The separate financial experiment uses walk-forward training with real distributional, reconstruction, and contrastive heads. Its R5fin records set \texttt{fixed\_aux=0}, so Table~\ref{tab:finance_repair} evaluates real auxiliaries. \section{Proofs and Additional Analysis}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Statements Deferred from the Main Text}
We formalize several statements discussed in the main text. Theorem numbering continues from the main text.


\begin{proposition}[Sufficient conditions for gradient vanishing]\label{prop:gradient_vanishing}
For a differentiable prediction map with Jacobian $J$ and output loss gradient $\ell$, the parameter gradient vanishes iff $J^\top\ell=0$. Sufficient conditions are $\ell=0$, or identical Jacobian rows together with $\mathbf1^\top\ell=0$. Equal prediction values alone imply neither condition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the chain rule. For a counterexample to the stronger assertion, $\hat y_i=\theta x_i$ is constant at $\theta=0$ but has Jacobian rows $x_i$. The pairwise logistic loss $\log(1+e^{-(\hat y_1-\hat y_2)})$ has output gradient $(-1/2,1/2)$ at a tie, so its parameter gradient is $(x_2-x_1)/2$, generally nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scope of positive attenuation]\label{prop:minimality}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:transition}, a fixed positive multiplier $q$ leaves a finite threshold $\alpha_c/q$ but can prevent the transition at a specified dose. A dose-dependent positive multiplier can keep the effective dose subcritical for every $\alpha$: for $0<\epsilon<1$, take $q(\alpha)=(1-\epsilon)/(1+\alpha/\alpha_c)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $q\alpha$ for $\alpha$. The proposed choice gives $q\alpha/\alpha_c=(1-\epsilon)\alpha/(\alpha+\alpha_c)<1$. A positive stable equilibrium remains, although its lower basin boundary persists. Preserving gradient direction therefore does not imply that positive task weighting must fail.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rank-dilution scaling]\label{prop:rank_dilution}
Assume the effective ratio scales as $\rho_\star\asymp(m_\Pi/m)^{\gamma_0}$, $\gamma_0\ge1$, and $m_\Pi\propto m^\beta$, $0\le\beta<1$. Then $\alpha_c=1/\rho_\star$ implies
\begin{equation}
\gamma \;=\; \gamma_0\,(1-\beta), \qquad \gamma_0 \ge 1,
\label{eq:rank_dilution}
\end{equation}
The separable scaling is assumed; rank alone does not imply it. More generally, restoration $\asymp m^a$ and contraction $\asymp m^b m_\Pi^c$ yield $\gamma=a-b-c\beta$, with the displayed form requiring $c=a-b=\gamma_0$. If the toxic norm scales as $m_\Pi^p$, then $c=2p$ only when contraction depends quadratically on that norm and has no additional rank dependence. The equality $\gamma_0=2p$ further requires $a-b=2p$. Neither exponent is identified by the toxic eigenvalue spectrum alone. A fixed effective rank gives $\beta=0$; a fixed per-head output dimension need not.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Composition of rank-one PSD operators]\label{cor:additive}
Let $T=\sum_k\rho_k u_ku_k^\top$, with unit $u_k$ and $\rho_k\ge0$, in common restoration-normalized coordinates. If the directions are mutually orthogonal, $\lambda_{\max}(T)=\max_k\rho_k$; if aligned, it is $\sum_k\rho_k$. An additional orthogonal direction leaves the maximum unchanged only if its coefficient does not exceed the previous maximum.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For orthogonal directions, the $u_k$ are eigenvectors. For aligned directions, the sum is a single rank-one operator. Neither case establishes the geometry of the production model's heads.
\end{proof}

For an explicit scalar example, take $b(\sigma)=\lambda\sigma^2/(1+\sigma^2)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\pm=\frac{\lambda\pm\sqrt{\lambda^2-4(\alpha\kappa)^2}}{2\alpha\kappa},\qquad \alpha_c=\lambda/(2\kappa).
\label{eq:fp}
\end{equation}
This verifies the fold and its basin dependence. Radial auxiliary behavior,
\begin{equation}
g_a=\kappa\delta h+o(\|\delta h\|),
\label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
requires isotropic curvature; stationarity at a constant representation is insufficient. For a general encoder, the Jacobian still determines its effect on variance through \eqref{eq:signed_variance}. In discrete time, linearizing a scalar stable equilibrium~\citep{strogatz1994nonlinear} with derivative $-\ell$ gives
\begin{equation}
e_{t+1}=(1-\eta\ell)e_t,\qquad 0<\eta\ell<2,
\label{eq:parallel_contraction}
\end{equation}
where $e_t$ is a deviation from that equilibrium, not total representation scatter.

Power-law spectra do not determine the capacity exponent. For eigenvalues $\lambda_i=C i^{-(1+s)}$ with $C$ independent of rank,
$\|A_r\|_F^2=C^2\sum_{i=1}^r i^{-2(1+s)}$. Hence the Frobenius norm is bounded for $s>-1/2$, grows as $\sqrt{\log r}$ for $s=-1/2$, and as $r^{-s-1/2}$ for $s<-1/2$. A decaying spectrum ($s>-1$) therefore has power exponent at most $1/2$; for the commonly intended $s>0$ it has exponent zero. The trace instead involves $\sum_i i^{-(1+s)}$, and the operator norm is $C$ for a decreasing spectrum. Rank-dependent normalization $C(r,m)$ changes these conclusions. Thus neither a universal $\gamma_0(s)$ nor $\gamma_0=2p$ follows without specifying the norm, normalization, restoration and coupling to dynamics. Concretely, if $\kappa\asymp m^b\|A_r\|^q$, $\|A_r\|\asymp r^p$, and $r\asymp m^\beta$, then $\gamma=a-b-qp\beta$. The restricted form $\gamma_0(1-\beta)$ requires $a-b=qp=\gamma_0$. Hence $\gamma_0=2p$ holds only under the additional quadratic coupling $q=2$ and matching restoration exponent $a-b=2p$; these are not implied by the measured width slope.

The original two-head generator has $W_k\in\mathbb R^{m\times r}$ with independent entries of variance $1/r$. Its combined span has rank $\min(m,2r)$ almost surely. For the averaged quadratic loss the sample-normalized Hessian is $A=2\sum_k W_kW_k^\top/(2r)$, with $\mathbb E A=2I/r$. At $r=32$, widths $32,64,128,256$ therefore have ranks $32,64,64,64$. Fixed per-head rank is not fixed combined rank over this range, and changing $r$ also changes curvature. The supplied orthonormal control instead constructs $A=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)Q^\top$ at exactly specified rank and normalization.

The RS1 experiment fixes per-head rank $r\in\{4,8,16\}$ across widths $32,48,64,96$, giving combined ranks $8,16,32$. At anchor $3\times10^{-4}$, descriptive half-height slopes are $-0.31$, $0.45$, and $3.02$; minimum relative scatter ranges are $0.040$--$0.068$, $0.013$--$0.018$, and $0.0056$--$0.0132$. Using the unpenalized anchor changes the slopes to $0.65$, $5.55$, and $3.88$. The fitted exponent is therefore sensitive to both rank and anchor. The analyzer retains all twelve curves and reports crossings and scatter floors separately.

The orthonormal control constructs the sample-normalized auxiliary Hessian $A=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)Q^\top$ at exact rank $r$ and verifies its amplitude. Three normalizations hold different quantities fixed: $\lambda=1$ fixes curvature per direction, so the Frobenius norm grows as $\sqrt r$; $\lambda=1/r$ fixes the trace; and $\lambda=1/\sqrt r$ fixes the Frobenius norm. The training grid tests the first two at $m\in\{32,64,128\}$, $r\in\{4,16\}$, seven doses, and three seeds, holding the loss, optimizer, and data generator otherwise fixed. Three observations follow. First, at fixed width and per-direction curvature, the $r=16$ crossing is lower than the $r=4$ crossing by factors of $4.8$ at $m=64$ and $228$ at $m=32$. Second, the fitted width slope for $r=4$ changes from $-0.29$ under $\lambda=1$ to $+0.91$ under $\lambda=1/r$. Third, minimum scatter is $3.5$--$11\%$ of the healthy reference for $r=4$, versus $0.4$--$1.1\%$ for $r=16$. Thus normalization affects the estimated exponent even when rank is controlled exactly. Each three-width fit has only one residual degree of freedom and cannot identify a precise exponent. The commands \texttt{controlled\_rank\_scan.py --self-test} and \texttt{run\_controlled\_rank\_grid.py} verify the construction and reproduce the twelve configurations. The historical squared pairwise hinge has zero margin, so tied scores can attain zero primary loss. These controls concern that objective; they do not establish the same transition for informative ranking objectives. The separate \texttt{--primary-loss logistic} option removes this degeneracy, but no unfinished logistic sweep is counted as evidence.


The synthetic testbed uses random toxic projections of rank $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ and a low-rank primary task with $2$--$8$ active directions across seeds (mean $5.3$). It sweeps 17 logarithmically spaced doses over $[3\times10^{-4},10]$ at six widths $m\in\{8,\dots,256\}$ under a pairwise-ranking loss. The toxic-rank schedule is proportional to width. The relative half-height rule gives $\gamma=0.07$ across six widths and $\gamma=0.01$ across the four widths used below. This nearly flat trend is consistent with the restricted law at $\beta=1$, but does not independently establish that law.

Using a fixed absolute scatter threshold instead gives $\gamma=1.53$ ($R^2=0.91$) on the same curves. At $\sigma_h=1.5\times10^{-3}$, the crossing depends on the width-dependent healthy amplitude: the fixed-rank family has $\sigma_h(0)\propto m^{0.95}$. The absolute rule also gives nearly constant slopes $1.94,2.02,2.04,1.96,1.89$ across rank schedules $\beta=0,\dots,1$, varying by only $0.15$ and largely masking schedule dependence. Using relative crossings, the fixed-per-head-rank family instead gives $\gamma_0=2.43$ ($R^2=0.90$, four widths). Its combined span can still grow as $\min(m,2r)$, so this is not an exact fixed-effective-rank estimate.

Both synthetic families extend the dose grid to $\alpha=0$ (21 points), using four widths $m\in\{32,64,128,256\}$, five seeds, and 800 epochs. The fixed-rank family is the $\beta=0$ case with constant per-head rank $r$. A corresponding ten-seed run yields crossings differing by $+3.1\%,+1.8\%,-5.8\%,+19.7\%$ across these widths. This comparison quantifies sensitivity to seed count, including variation in the healthy anchor at larger widths.

Two checks qualify the crossing estimator. First, a half-height crossing can occur without near-total collapse. For fixed per-head rank $r\le4$, scatter plateaus at $2$--$23\%$ of its healthy value; only $r\ge8$ reaches a floor of at most $0.9\%$. These floors must be distinguished from the operational $0.3$ collapse threshold. Second, the synthetic fits use the first penalized dose, $\alpha=3\times10^{-4}$, as the anchor. The unpenalized value is set by the ranking loss, is shared across toxic ranks, and lies $30$--$40\%$ above the penalized trend. Varying the anchor over $\alpha\in[0,3\times10^{-3}]$ preserves the decreasing ordering of $\gamma(\beta)$: $\gamma_0\in[1.55,3.23]$ at $\beta=0$, versus $\gamma(1)\in[-0.06,+0.74]$. The ordering is more stable than the fitted coefficients.

The rank-schedule fits test a conditional scaling hypothesis. Given $\alpha_c\asymp(m/m_\Pi)^{\gamma_0}$ and an independently measured $m_\Pi\asymp m^\beta$, substitution gives $\gamma=\gamma_0(1-\beta)$. Testing the premise requires measuring effective rank separately from the observed slope. The generator audit above specifies how per-head and combined ranks differ.

Crossing rules affect the fitted magnitudes. For HAR, an absolute threshold $T\in[0.20,0.50]$ gives $\gamma\in[0.66,0.82]$, compared with $0.66$ under the four-width relative rule. Battery requires a grid that reaches a healthy branch: earlier sweeps started at $\alpha=300$, where relative scatter was already $0.07$--$0.11$, making thresholds left-censored. Extending to $\alpha\in[10^{-4},300]$ with five seeds gives a healthy branch at $\alpha\le10^{-3}$ (relative scatter $1.000\pm0.005$) and crossings $0.95,0.89,0.90,1.18$ at $m=32,\dots,256$. The fitted slope is $\gamma_{\mathrm{bat}}=0.10$ ($R^2=0.43$), varying over $[-0.10,+0.12]$ across crossing rules. These four widths are consistent with weak width dependence but do not resolve a precise exponent.

Treating each width's crossing as one observation gives four-width OLS 95\% intervals $[-0.07,+4.93]$ for the $\beta=0$ schedule, $[-0.16,+1.49]$ for HAR, $[-0.24,+0.43]$ for battery, and $[-1.16,+1.20]$ for RadioML. None excludes zero. Interior schedules yield positive slopes: $2.57$ (CI $[0.87,4.27]$) at $\beta=0.25$ and $1.86$ ($[0.81,2.91]$) at $\beta=0.50$; $\beta=1$ remains consistent with zero. At the reported anchor, all four remaining schedule intervals contain $\gamma_0(1-\beta)$, whereas the $\beta=0.75$ interval excludes it at anchor $10^{-4}$. These results support schedule dependence without identifying an anchor-independent coefficient.

The denser SYN8 family supplies $26$ completed $(m,\beta)$ cells, ten seeds each. Since $r(64)=32$ for every schedule, we fit $\log\alpha_c=c+\gamma_0(1-\beta)\log(m/64)$. At anchor $3\times10^{-4}$, the constrained fit gives $\gamma_0=2.50$ (nominal OLS $95\%$ CI $[1.94,3.06]$). Across anchors $\{10^{-4},3\times10^{-4}\}$ and inclusion/exclusion of $m=128$, all four conditional intervals exclude zero (lowest bound $1.04$). In the unconstrained model $c+a x+b\beta x$, $x=\log(m/64)$, every interval for $a+b$ contains zero and $-b/a=0.78$--$1.03$: the linear form is not rejected, not established. These are cell-level fit intervals, not independent-seed uncertainty: the schedules share seeds and the $m=64$ curves are identical. Joint/anchor-row point estimates range from $1.35$ to $2.87$. At $m\ge96$ the sampled curve never resolves the collapsed floor; half-height crossings interpolate the observed decline, not a verified collapse threshold. Thus SYN8 sharpens a conditional trend without supplying a precise physical exponent. \texttt{synth\_beta\_joint.py} reproduces the fits.

Nine-width HAR and battery sweeps use $m\in\{16,24,32,48,64,96,128,192,256\}$ with the same driver, seeds, epochs, and dose grids. Shared configurations agree to floating-point precision (maximum difference $2\times10^{-16}$). HAR gives $\gamma_{\mathrm{HAR}}=0.38$ (95\% CI $[+0.10,+0.67]$, $R^2=0.60$, $n=9$); excluding the two narrowest widths gives $0.60$. Battery gives $\gamma_{\mathrm{bat}}=-0.06$ (CI $[-0.17,+0.06]$), consistent with weak width dependence. The denser grid narrows conditional fit intervals but does not eliminate crossing uncertainty. For example, HAR at $m=32$, $\alpha=10^{-3}$ has relative scatter $0.5026\pm0.0524$, placing the half-height crossing close to a sampled grid point and making interpolation sensitive to seed variation. We report both four- and nine-width estimates rather than treating either as an identified dynamical exponent.

We also examine probe scaling across encoders. Repeating calibration with a Transformer at $m\in\{32,64,128\}$ and ten seeds gives probe exponents $-0.93$ for HAR ($R^2=0.99$), $+0.39$ for battery ($R^2=0.87$), and $-0.17$ for RadioML ($R^2=0.79$). The corresponding MLP values are $-1.63$ ($R^2=0.96$), $+0.67$ ($R^2=0.86$), and $+0.28$ ($R^2=0.40$). Signs agree for HAR and battery; RadioML is consistent with zero for both encoders, and the 95\% intervals overlap in all three domains. Probe exponents and scatter-crossing exponents estimate different quantities. Their MLP point estimates agree in sign for RadioML ($+0.28$ versus $+0.02$), but disagree for HAR ($-1.63$ versus $+0.38$) and battery ($+0.67$ versus $-0.06$). A half-height crossing locates a dose at which trained scatter reaches half its reference value; the probe estimates $1/\hat\rho$ at the warm-up anchor. Agreement within a factor of a few at one width (a factor of $7$ for NYUv2 at $m=64$) does not imply agreement in width scaling. Negative probe slopes do not satisfy the restricted nonnegative exponent assumed in \eqref{eq:rank_dilution}, but that assumption is not a general constraint on empirical slopes. The width analysis here therefore reports crossing exponents separately, while Appendix~\ref{app:nyu} reports probe values as calibration statistics.

Several sweeps show bimodal per-seed scatter near the empirical crossing. Such outcomes are compatible with a saddle-node model, but can also arise from initialization, finite training time, and optimization. We report the heterogeneity without assigning it a unique dynamical cause.

\section{Generalizations and Finite-Size Theory}\label{app:general}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed attenuation in a specified model]\label{prop:attenuation}
For $T\succeq0$ in restoration-normalized coordinates, uniform attenuation $T\mapsto(1-\lambda)T$, $0\le\lambda<1$, multiplies the first-modal-contraction threshold by $1/(1-\lambda)$. At fixed dose the smallest $\lambda$ meeting $\alpha(1-\lambda)\|T\|\le1$ is $\max(0,1-1/(\alpha\|T\|))$ when $\alpha\|T\|>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All eigenvalues scale by $1-\lambda$; solve the displayed inequality. This statement concerns a fixed PSD model, not arbitrary signed or nonsymmetric filtered dynamics.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Annihilation by orthogonal compression]\label{thm:filters}
Let $T\succeq0$, $U=\mathrm{range}(T)$, and $Q=I-P_S$ be an orthogonal projector. Then $QTQ=0$ iff $U\subseteq S$. Among such deletions the unique smallest deleted subspace is $U$, and $I-P_U$ preserves its orthogonal complement. This is an algebraic statement about two-sided compression.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$QTQ=(T^{1/2}Q)^\top(T^{1/2}Q)=0$ iff $\mathrm{range}(Q)\subseteq\ker T$, equivalently $U\subseteq S$. A general one-sided backward operator $QT$ need not describe symmetric neural-network dynamics; a signed filter can even reverse contraction, so its operator norm cannot supply the missing implication.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subspace estimation bound]\label{cor:estimation}
If $\|P_U-P_{\hat U}\|\le\epsilon$ and $Q=I-P_{\hat U}$, then
\begin{equation}
\|QT\|\le\epsilon\|T\|,\qquad \|QTQ\|\le\epsilon^2\|T\|.
\label{eq:estimation}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $T=P_UTP_U$ and $\|QP_U\|\le\epsilon$ on one or both sides. Whitening must precede this argument if $T$ is restoration-normalized; an angle bound in other coordinates is insufficient. These are upper bounds on residual magnitude, not evidence of a nonzero residual or inevitable failure at a finite dose.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimum rank for a PSD compression]\label{thm:minimalrank}
Fix a PSD operator $T$ in specified Euclidean coordinates, with eigenvalues $\kappa_1\ge\cdots\ge\kappa_d\ge0$, and a threshold $\tau\ge0$. Among orthogonal projectors $Q=I-P_S$ satisfying $\|QTQ\|\le\tau$, the minimum deletion rank is
\[
r^\star=\#\{j:\kappa_j>\tau\}.
\]
Deleting $S_c=\mathrm{span}\{u_j:\kappa_j>\tau\}$ attains it. Within the class of spectral deletions, $S_c$ also minimizes any inclusion-monotone deleted energy. Uniqueness of the energy optimum requires positive energy in every additionally deleted eigendirection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The spectral choice leaves only eigenvalues at most $\tau$. For any $\dim S<r^\star$, dimension counting gives a unit vector $v\in S^\perp\cap S_c$. Its Rayleigh quotient satisfies $v^\top QTQv=v^\top Tv>\tau$, violating feasibility. For spectral deletions feasibility requires containing $S_c$, giving the energy statement by inclusion. Rotated subspaces can also attain the same minimum rank; no global minimum-energy claim over rotations follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Saturation and its limits]\label{cor:saturated}
For $\tau=1/\alpha$, the optimal rank changes in steps as $\alpha$ crosses inverse positive eigenvalues and eventually equals $\dim U$. The limiting projector is $I-P_U$, which equals full detachment only on $U$, or globally when $U$ is the entire gradient space. Exhausting a finite probe depth does not prove saturation of $U$.
\end{corollary}

The theorem concerns a specified operator, not a mean of gradient-norm ratios. For a linear reconstruction decoder $Wh$ with squared loss, $g_a=W^\top\delta$ (up to loss normalization) and the Hessian is $W^\top W$. The uncentered second moment relevant to normalized residual energy is
\[
C_g=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{g_ag_a^\top}{(\|g_p\|+\varepsilon)^2}\right]
=W^\top\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\delta\delta^\top}{(\|g_p\|+\varepsilon)^2}\right]W.
\]
For any rank-$r$ projector $P$, the squared residual is $\operatorname{tr}((I-P)C_g)$, minimized by the top $r$ eigenvectors of $C_g$ (the Rayleigh--Ritz principle). Its square root upper-bounds the corresponding mean norm by Jensen's inequality. It does not generally minimize that mean itself. Hessian eigenvectors coincide with this basis under isotropic weighted residual covariance; representation PCA requires its leading subspace to coincide with the leading eigenspace of $C_g$. Commutation gives a common eigenbasis but does not ensure the same ordering. A nonlinear decoder additionally contributes residual-weighted second derivatives to its Hessian. Thus a nearly flat PCA residual curve is consistent with gradient energy outside leading representation components, but does not prove that the reconstruction residual is orthogonal to those components.

Forward and backward-only filters differ even when their Jacobians agree. A complementary experimental intervention uses $v=\nabla_\theta V$, proposed update $u_0=G_p+\alpha G_a$, and slack $h=V-V_{\min}$. For $k>0$ and $v\ne0$, the closest update satisfying $v^\top u\le kh$ is
\[

\]

\begin{proposition}[Sampling fluctuation of a frozen statistic]\label{prop:finitebatch}
For independent identically distributed scalar diagnostics $X_i$ with finite variance $\sigma_X^2$, $\hat\rho=B^{-1}\sum_iX_i$ has variance $\sigma_X^2/B$ and obeys a central limit theorem. Under a normal approximation the probability that the \emph{gate} $\alpha\hat\rho>1$ opens is $\Phi(\sqrt B(\rho-1/\alpha)/\sigma_X)$. This is not the probability of training collapse. Batch-dependent centering or fitted bases require additional analysis or an independent frozen calibration sample.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use variance additivity and the central limit theorem. For a ratio $A/P$, the delta-method variance includes $-2\mu_A\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)/\mu_P^3$ as well as the two marginal variance terms. Mean per-example ratios and ratios of means are distinct estimands.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional calibration bound]\label{prop:calibration}
Assume independent $X_i\in[0,M]$ with common mean $\rho$ and population variance at most $v_0$. Bernstein's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb P(|\hat\rho-\rho|\ge\varepsilon)\le2\exp\left[-\frac{B\varepsilon^2}{2v_0+2M\varepsilon/3}\right].
\label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
Thus an error probability at most $\delta$ follows if
\begin{equation}
B\ge(2v_0+2M\varepsilon/3)\varepsilon^{-2}\log(2/\delta).
\label{eq:samplesize}
\end{equation}
A nonzero primary-gradient denominator alone does not provide a uniform bound $M$. Population bounds cannot be replaced by observed sample moments without a separate valid empirical-concentration result.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the bounded-variable Bernstein inequality to the centered independent sum and solve for $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Certifying a frozen gate or nested rank rule]\label{cor:certificate}
Under Proposition~\ref{prop:calibration}, a gate with population margin $d=|\rho-1/\alpha|>0$ has error probability at most $2\exp[-Bd^2/(2v_0+2Md/3)]$. For $R+1$ fixed residual statistics with uniformly positive margins, a union bound controls all their threshold decisions and hence the selected nested rank. This does not identify the statistics with Hessian eigenvalues, certify an adaptively fitted basis without conditioning, or certify a subsequent training trajectory.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A wrong threshold decision requires an estimation error at least its margin; apply the bound and then the union bound. Strict margins exclude equality cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Local linear noise floor]\label{prop:lyapunov}
For deviations $e_{t+1}=a e_t+\eta\xi_t$ from a fixed equilibrium, assume $|a|<1$ and independent mean-zero innovations of variance $D$. The stationary variance is $\eta^2D/(1-a^2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The second-moment recursion is $\mathbb E e_{t+1}^2=a^2\mathbb E e_t^2+\eta^2D$; sum the convergent geometric series. This is variance of deviations, not total representation scatter. Divergence of a local linear approximation as $|a|\to1$ does not prove a universal ordering of half-height and binary-collapse thresholds in a nonlinear experiment.
\end{proof}

Table~\ref{tab:certificate} compares observed fluctuations with plug-in envelopes based on estimated variance and range. Shared batch centering and estimated moments limit these envelopes to diagnostic use. The records also distinguish mean per-example ratios from ratios of batch-mean gradients: the latter can vanish for centered variance losses.

\subsection{Prediction Variance Normalization and Target \texorpdfstring{$z$}{z}-Scoring}\label{app:zscore}

For $\hat y=w^\top h+b$, shrinking centered features by $1/c$ and expanding $w$ by $c$ preserves predictions. Shrinking both changes the regression loss, and constant predictions generally cannot fit varying targets. This scale ambiguity motivates reporting prediction variance and across-example feature covariance alongside task error, and testing prediction normalization and target scaling as controls.

The historical regression recipe standardizes targets and divides predictions by a detached batch standard deviation. This changes optimization and can reduce sensitivity to scale, but detached normalization does not implement the derivative of a truly scale-invariant loss. Softmax also does not normalize logit scale: changing that scale changes probabilities. These operations therefore require empirical ablations, with scaling statistics fit only on training data.

\begin{algorithmic}[1]

A four-seed MATR ablation removes each weighting factor in turn. Omitting the rate factor gives fast-charge SOH RMSE $0.95$; omitting the temperature factor gives extreme-temperature SOH RMSE $2.35$. Table~\ref{tab:compose} reports the composition experiment. On HAR, accuracy changes from $0.943$ with filtering alone to $0.933$ with PCGrad and $0.940$ with CAGrad. Relative scatter is at most $0.15$ without filtering, compared with $0.97$--$1.14$ after filtering. These measurements support compatibility for the tested filter-then-combiner order.

The synthetic testbed uses 17 log-spaced doses, six widths, and random projections of rank $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Its near-flat relative-crossing slope ($\gamma=0.07$) contrasts with the absolute-threshold estimate ($1.53$), as analyzed above. In RadioML, a five-seed sweep over $\alpha\in[3\times10^{-3},1]$, refined near the transition, gives crossings $\{0.17,0.39,0.24,0.22\}$ at widths $\{32,64,128,256\}$. The eightfold width range changes the crossing by at most a factor of $2.3$, much less than the factor of eight implied by $\gamma=1$. At $m=64$, per-seed crossings span $0.29$--$0.54$. Together with the near-zero fit $R^2$, this variation limits inference about the width slope.

The repository supplies the scripts and recorded results listed in its reproduction manifest; data-dependent reruns additionally require the stated datasets and preprocessing. New audits are identified separately from historical experiments.
The drivers \texttt{cross\_domain\_mtl.py}, \texttt{battery\_bmtl\_v3.py}, and \texttt{nyu\_bmtl.py} implement the STF family for the unified, battery, and dense-perception experiments. Algorithm~\ref{alg:stfcal} specifies the calibrated forward maps; each driver records application-specific details. PCGrad, CAGrad, and FAMO share these experiment pipelines, including the composition study. The remaining methods (MGDA, Aligned-MTL, Nash-MTL, IMTL-G, IMTL-L, GradNorm, and uncertainty weighting) use \texttt{moo\_combiners.py}, which ports kernels from the authors' reference implementations. Comparisons use the recorded optimizer and schedule with published method defaults; batch-size exceptions are stated below.

JSON job manifests specify each experimental configuration by domain, width, mode, and seed, together with its command, output path, and log path. A deterministic runner skips existing outputs. The manifests cover collapse scans, width sweeps, filter composition, real auxiliary heads, battery weight sweeps, calibrated validation, NYUv2 experiments, and the three-domain multi-task baseline comparison.
Experiments use fixed integer seeds, with counts reported for each study. Where available, JSON records retain individual seed outcomes; aggregate-only historical records are identified in the artifact audit.

The original unified runs use an NVIDIA A100 80\,GB, Adam ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$), bf16 autocast, and TF32 matrix multiplication. Calibration runs in fp32 outside autocast on detached features. Batch size is $256$ for the unified protocol, real-head comparison, regularizer sweep, and MATR application; $128$ for NASA conflict baselines versus $256$ for its plain-summation runs; $32$ for NYUv2; and $512$ for financial walk-forward training. Individual scripts and JSON configurations record these optimizer differences.

\section{NYUv2: Dense Perception Beyond Time Series}\label{app:nyu}

NYUv2~\citep{silberman2012indoor,ladicky2014pulling} contains 795 training and 654 test scenes with semantic segmentation (13 classes), depth, and surface normals. Images are resized to $144\times192$; segmentation pixels labeled $-1$ and zero-norm normal targets are masked. The convolutional encoder uses four stride-2 blocks with batch normalization and ReLU ($3\to32\to64\to128\to256$ channels), followed by a $d=128$ representation and linear task heads. Losses are cross-entropy, $L_1$ depth error, and cosine normal error. We report final-epoch pixel accuracy, mIoU, depth RMSE/MAE, and mean angular error over ten seeds, without test-based checkpoint selection. The \emph{proj} auxiliary is a fixed random variance-shrinkage projection to $d/4$, evaluated at $\alpha=1$. The \emph{ae} auxiliary is a trainable three-layer input-reconstruction head, evaluated at $\alpha=250$, or $3.4$ times its probe-based threshold. A separate unified-protocol experiment uses an MLP and a $9\times12$ coarse depth grid (108 outputs), with the optimizer, probe, and collapse criterion of Section~\ref{sec:unified_protocol}.

After four detached warm-up epochs, the unified MLP probe gives $\hat\rho=2.26\pm0.16$, $2.01\pm0.10$, $1.64\pm0.09$, and $1.29\pm0.05$ at widths $32,64,128,256$. Per-seed ranges are $2.06$--$2.65$, $1.90$--$2.20$, $1.51$--$1.77$, and $1.22$--$1.38$. The inverse-mean calibration values are $0.44$, $0.50$, $0.61$, and $0.78$, yielding a probe-based width slope of $0.27$ ($R^2=0.98$, 95\% CI $[+0.15,+0.40]$).

This is not the scatter-crossing exponent. Half-height crossings on the same widths yield $\gamma=-0.77$; varying the crossing level from $0.3$ to $0.7$ gives $[-0.94,-0.73]$ with $R^2\ge0.99$, whereas HAR remains positive ($+0.34$ to $+0.46$). At $m=64$, the crossing and calibration values are $0.069$ and $0.50$, a factor of $7$ apart. Extending the grid four decades lower restores relative scatter to $0.99$--$1.01$ at $\alpha=10^{-6}$, compared with $0.64$--$0.83$ at the previous lowest dose $0.01$, without materially changing the crossing. The opposite width trends therefore cannot be explained solely by the original grid's lower endpoint.

We report the probe because it determines the implemented gate, while retaining the crossing estimate as a separate diagnostic. The probe slope varies over a range of $0.17$ across calibration times on NYUv2, versus $2.55$ on HAR (Appendix~\ref{app:warmup}); this relative stability does not make it a capacity exponent. For the convolutional encoder, the \emph{proj} probe gives $\hat\rho=3.27\pm0.54$ (range $2.45$--$3.92$), predicting approximately $0.31$, while the \emph{ae} probe gives $0.0136\pm0.0015$, predicting approximately $73$.

Table~\ref{tab:nyu_dose} shows joint collapse in $10/10$ seeds for every tested $\alpha\ge0.5$. Calibrated filtering achieves $0/10$ only for $\alpha\ge1$, when all gates open and relative scatter is approximately $1.00$; the recorded candidate rank is 3. At $\alpha=0.5$, the gate opens in the five seeds with $\hat\rho>2$, and all five avoid collapse (candidate rank 1). This is an empirical threshold comparison, subject to Corollary~\ref{cor:certificate}'s scope restrictions. At lower doses, closed-gate outcomes are close to joint training.

The task metric can conceal shrinkage: joint depth RMSE falls overall from $1.335$ to $1.092$ (approximately $18\%$) while relative scatter falls from $0.62$ to $0.04$. Thus the high-dose joint model has lower depth error than the filtered models despite meeting the representation-collapse criterion. At every tested $\alpha\ge1$, paired Wilcoxon tests on relative scatter give $p\approx2\times10^{-3}$, the smallest two-sided value attainable with ten nonzero, same-sign paired differences.

Ten-seed calibrated experiments support Section~\ref{sec:stfcal}. At high doses, joint versus \textsc{stfcal} relative scatter is $0.016$ versus $0.990$ for HAR ($\alpha=100$), $0.147$ versus $1.001$ for battery ($\alpha=100$), and $0.014$ versus $0.982$ for RadioML ($\alpha=3000$). In each case, collapse rates are $10/10$ versus $0/10$. At low doses with closed gates, outcomes are close: HAR at $\alpha=0.001$ gives $0.525$ versus $0.528$, and battery at $\alpha=0.01$ gives $0.963$ versus $0.965$.

With Transformer encoders at $\alpha=100$, HAR scatter changes from $0.030$ to $1.041$ and battery scatter from $0.036$ to $1.000$, again reducing collapse from $10/10$ to $0/10$. Battery SOH RMSE improves from $0.447$ to $0.267$. On CelebA at $\alpha=100$ (five seeds), scatter increases from $0.003$ to $0.934$ and collapse falls from $5/5$ to $0/5$.

The real-head experiments use $m=64$ and ten seeds (Table~\ref{tab:real_aux}). Neither reconstruction head collapses at its tested dose; filtering has little effect or modestly reduces primary accuracy. The training-subject classifier presents a different trade-off: joint training achieves identity accuracy $0.646$ against chance $1/21$, while filtering reduces it to $0.055$ and improves primary accuracy. This is a training-head measurement, not a held-out privacy attack. For high-dose SNR regression, joint training collapses in every seed; hard and soft filtering do not restore accuracy, and detachment performs best among the tested interventions. Probe magnitudes differ substantially across these heads, but the false negatives discussed in Section~\ref{sec:real_aux} preclude a universal decision rule. Figure~\ref{fig:battery_repair} shows the battery repair outcome, Table~\ref{tab:unified_repair} the corresponding collapse and recovery rates, and Table~\ref{tab:related} the intervention families.

The results identify several distinct regimes: task error can improve as scatter shrinks, real auxiliaries can remain benign over the tested grid, and calibration can miss contraction. Frozen-basis error and sampling variation provide additional diagnostic checks. Numerical instability and loss of plasticity~\citep{dohare2024loss} remain outside the fixed-operator model.

The battery encoder maps a per-cycle window $x\in\mathbb R^{T\times D}$ of normalized voltage, current, temperature, and resistance to $h\in\mathbb R^d$. It combines a \textsc{TSMixer} feature/temporal-mixing block~\citep{ekambaram2023tsmixer} with a gated state-space block~\citep{gu2022s4,gu2023mamba}. SOH ($L_2$), RUL ($L_1$), reconstruction, and distributional heads share this representation. The real auxiliary heads are evaluated empirically and are not assumed to satisfy Definition~\ref{def:degmin}.

RUL targets require careful handling of right censoring. If a cell has not reached end of life when recording stops, its remaining lifetime is known only to exceed a lower bound. Treating that bound as an exact target can label a healthy cell with zero remaining life. In MATR, $59.2\%$ of samples are censored and $96/185$ cells do not reach EOL within their records. Initialization zeros in summary arrays cause a further $48/185$ cells to receive an EOL index of zero under a naive threshold search. Restricting the search to each trajectory's physically valid suffix recovers these cells. We retain censored cells and use $|\hat r-r|$ for observed lifetimes and $\max(0,r_{\mathrm{lb}}-\hat r)$ for censored lower bounds. This one-sided loss is motivated by censored regression~\citep{tobin1958estimation}, but is not a Tobit likelihood. Excluding cells without observed EOL would leave only 14 test cells in the historical split (Section~\ref{sec:bmtl_ablation}).

To emphasize fast-charge, extreme-temperature, and rapid-degradation windows, BatteryMTL uses three multiplicative weighting factors:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w_i = &\;w_i^{(\mathrm{rate})}\;\cdot\;w_i^{(\mathrm{temp})}\;\cdot\;w_i^{(\mathrm{degrad})},\\
w_i^{(\mathrm{rate})} = &\;1+\omega\,\mathbf{1}[\text{C-rate}_i>r_q],\\
w_i^{(\mathrm{temp})} = &\;1+\omega\,\mathbf{1}[T_i<t_{lo}\vee T_i>t_{hi}],\\
w_i^{(\mathrm{degrad})} = &\;1+\omega\,\tfrac{|\Delta\text{SOH}_i|}{\max_j|\Delta\text{SOH}_j|},\\
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:triweight}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:triweight} amplifies windows in which several factors are elevated, while also up-weighting windows with a single elevated factor. Thresholds are estimated from the training fold: $r_q$ is the 70th C-rate percentile, and $t_{lo},t_{hi}$ are the 10th and 90th temperature percentiles. These relative thresholds distinguish regimes within MATR, where $99.8\%$ of cycles exceed $1.5$C and temperatures lie within $31$--$36^\circ$C. In contrast, fixed cuts at $1.5$C and $0/40^\circ$C would mark nearly all cycles for rate and none for temperature. Section~\ref{sec:bmtl_ablation} reports regime-specific ablations; SOH-only and RUL-only models provide task-specific references, not guaranteed performance ceilings.

\subsection{Composition and Regime Weighting}

The following measurements expand the composition and weighting results in Sections~\ref{sec:bmtl} and~\ref{sec:conflict_ablations}.

At the tested high doses, applying PCGrad or CAGrad to the unfiltered battery branch leaves collapse rate at $1.0$ and relative scatter at $0.029/0.030$ (Table~\ref{tab:compose}). Hard filtering alone reduces collapse to $0.0$, restores relative scatter to $1.00$, and improves log-SOH RMSE from $0.611$ to $0.294$. Adding PCGrad or CAGrad afterward gives RMSE $0.283/0.291$ and scatter up to $1.18$. HAR and RadioML show the same qualitative preservation of scatter under composition. These results establish compatibility for the tested order; they neither show that sequencing is necessary nor establish why the unfiltered combiners fail.

BatteryMTL's multiplicative regime weights use training-data percentiles for C-rate and temperature, together with degradation rate. The aggregate SOH results change little, while factor ablations associate improvements in fast-charge and extreme-temperature windows with the rate and temperature terms, respectively (errors $0.95$ and $2.35$ when the corresponding terms are removed). Table~\ref{tab:bmtl} reports the historical aggregate results.

\subsection{Probe Dynamics During Training}\label{app:online}

The frozen calibration in Section~\ref{sec:stfcal} is motivated by the behavior of a continuously recomputed probe. We repeat unfiltered joint training with \texttt{--rho-trace}, using five seeds per configuration over 1730 HAR steps and 350 battery steps. Monitoring does not alter the measured trajectories: all 28 shared \textsc{bscale} configurations match exactly in task metric, collapse rate, relative scatter, and $\hat\rho$ (maximum difference zero).

Three observations matter. First, HAR probe values rise sharply during training: from $0.017$ to $1.9\times10^5$ at $m=32$, $0.010$ to $1.0\times10^5$ at $m=64$, and $0.006$ to $3.5\times10^4$ at $m=256$. Second, similar terminal values occur when the toxifier is inactive ($\alpha=0$) and at the lowest positive dose ($10^{-4}$), so a large probe value alone does not establish auxiliary-induced contraction. At $m=32$, increasing the dose instead lowers the terminal probe, to $5.3\times10^2$ at $10^{-2}$ and $1.1\times10^2$ at $3\times10^{-2}$. Third, in ten HAR configurations whose final scatter lies between $0.50$ and $1.04$ of the reference, the gate $\alpha\hat\rho\ge1$ opens in every seed between steps 110 and 590. A gate using this running statistic therefore activates on runs that do not meet the collapse criterion at the recorded endpoint.

Battery behaves differently: probe values remain $O(1)$, ranging from $0.29$--$0.49$ initially to $0.3$--$2.9$ at the final step. Among 35 seeds whose final scatter lies above the half-height level, the gate opens only once, at the last step. These domain-dependent trajectories motivate a specified calibration time; they do not establish that a fixed probe is universally preferable to every adaptive rule. The script \texttt{repro/scripts/rho\_trace\_analyze.py} reproduces both analyses.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Calibration Time}\label{app:warmup}

The default calibration measures $\hat\rho$ after four detached warm-up epochs and fixes the gate and rank. We vary only this measurement time: $W\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ at five widths $32$--$512$ for HAR and battery, and $W\in\{1,4,16,32\}$ at four widths for NYUv2, with ten seeds per cell. The $W=4$ configuration exactly reproduces all 100 shared HAR and battery probe values. NYUv2 gives $2.258/2.008/1.637/1.285$, matching the rounded values in Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}.

Probe values depend on the training state. Across the warm-up sweep, HAR at $m=32$ increases from $0.63$ to $6.9\times10^4$, and at $m=512$ from $51.9$ to $1.6\times10^4$. Battery remains $O(1)$: $0.96$ to $1.92$ at $m=32$ and $0.058$ to $0.67$ at $m=512$. NYUv2 at $m=32$ changes from $1.94$ to $4.19$, a factor of $2.2$. Accordingly, width slopes fitted to $1/\hat\rho$ vary from $-2.00$ to $+0.55$ for HAR, $+0.37$ to $+0.97$ for battery, and $+0.11$ to $+0.27$ for NYUv2. These are calibration-dependent probe slopes, not interchangeable estimates of capacity scaling. Differences in primary-gradient magnitude, which appears in the denominator, can contribute to this sensitivity.

Measurement time also changes gate activation. For HAR at $m=64$, the inverse-mean probe value is $1.34$ at $W=1$, $0.181$ at $W=2$, $9.07\times10^{-3}$ at $W=4$, and $1.7\times10^{-5}$ at $W=32$, compared with the measured transition interval $[0.001,0.01]$. In the sixty-epoch, ten-seed experiment at $\alpha=0.03$, gates remain closed for $W=1$ and $W=2$: both settings collapse in $10/10$ seeds, with accuracy $0.947$ and relative scatter $0.19/0.20$, close to joint training ($0.949$, $0.19$). For $W\ge4$, all gates open, no seed collapses, and scatter returns to $0.99$--$1.00$. At $\alpha=100$, all six warm-up settings activate the gate and avoid collapse.

A short warm-up therefore fails at the marginal dose in this experiment. Detached warm-up also changes the training trajectory, so closed-gate runs need not equal joint training from initialization. Checking whether $\alpha\hat\rho\ge1$ verifies activation at the chosen operating dose; it does not independently validate protection. The script \texttt{repro/scripts/warmup\_sensitivity.py} reproduces these results.

\subsection{Readout-Nullspace Protection with a Useful Real Auxiliary}\label{app:benefit_confirmation}

The old consumer applies $z_0(x)=W_0D_0^{-1}(h_0(x)-\mu_0)+b_0$, with fixed positive diagonal $D_0=\operatorname{diag}(s_0)$. Let $A=W_0D_0^{-1}$ and $P_\perp=I-A^\dagger A$, the orthogonal projector onto $\ker A$. We use established projection and backward-compatibility ideas~\citep{farajtabar2020ogd,shen2020backward} as a constructive control for measuring retained benefit and compatibility.

\begin{proposition}[Compatibility and remaining feature freedom]\label{prop:consumer_nullspace}
For any updated encoder $h_\theta$ and retained deterministic reference encoder $h_0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde h_\theta(x)=h_0(x)+P_\perp\bigl(h_\theta(x)-h_0(x)\bigr).
 \label{eq:consumer_nullspace}
\end{equation}
The frozen affine consumer has exactly the reference logits on every input in real arithmetic. It leaves $m-\operatorname{rank}(A)$ feature directions free. It is also the unique Euclidean-nearest feature vector to $h_\theta(x)$ among all vectors with the reference logits. Conversely, every feature displacement preserving these logits belongs to $\ker A$; if $A$ has full column rank, no nonzero compatible displacement remains.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $AP_\perp=0$, the logit difference is $AP_\perp(h_\theta-h_0)=0$. Rank--nullity gives the free dimension and the converse. The construction is the orthogonal projection onto the affine set $h_0(x)+\ker A$, proving the minimum-distance statement. The fixed normalizer's mean and the head's bias cancel in the difference.
\end{proof}

The row-vector implementation is $\widetilde H=H_0+(H_\theta-H_0)P_\perp$. A float64 SVD of the normalized head $A$ fixes the projector before continuation. Because the reference branch remains present, the logit identity holds for later encoder parameters and arbitrary inputs in real arithmetic. It also preserves fixed post-processing and its resulting NLL and ECE. Corpus-level float64 checks measure the numerical residual. This functional identity is stronger than the checkpoint-local flow statement and covers a different object from the sampled disagreement bound.

\subsection{Folding Compatibility into a Single Encoder}\label{app:compiled_interface}

The reference branch makes compatibility expensive. Freezing the nonlinear trunk lets us fold the constraint into its final affine map, retaining ordinary inference shapes but restricting feature learning. A new plan, committed after the earlier confirmation and before execution, tests this tradeoff as a separate study.

\begin{corollary}[Foldable affine compatibility]\label{cor:compiled_interface}
Let $h_0(x)=B_0\phi_0(x)+c_0$, with a retained deterministic trunk $\phi_0$. For arbitrary trainable $B,c$, set
\begin{equation}
 \overline B=B_0+P_\perp(B-B_0),\qquad
 \overline c=c_0+P_\perp(c-c_0).
 \label{eq:compiled_affine}
\end{equation}
Then $\overline h(x)=\overline B\phi_0(x)+\overline c$ has the reference affine-consumer logits for every input in real arithmetic. Deploying $\overline B,\overline c$ needs one trunk and one final affine map of the original shapes, with no reference branch or projection. If the trunk has $d$ outputs, this parameterization leaves $(m-\operatorname{rank}A)(d+1)$ affine degrees of freedom. Changing the trunk is outside this guarantee.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both $A(\overline B-B_0)$ and $A(\overline c-c_0)$ vanish. Folding is a substitution of one weight and bias, not a second inference operation. Each of the $d+1$ weight/bias columns can vary in $\ker A$.
\end{proof}

The construction uses constrained affine adaptation, related to nullspace updates and mergeable frozen-backbone methods~\citep{farajtabar2020ogd,pham2026ness,hu2022lora}. With a linear current head it remains a linear predictor of fixed trunk features. Folding preserves the constrained function exactly; whether that function retains auxiliary benefit is the empirical question.

\subsection{A Compatible Nonlinear Channel: Development Gate and Limits}\label{app:nonlinear_interface}

A narrow side network allows nonlinear learning while the base remains fixed. We combine side-tuning~\citep{zhang2020sidetuning} with established compatibility projections~\citep{farajtabar2020ogd,shen2020backward}:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde h(x)=f_0(x)+P_\perp g_\psi(x),\qquad
 A\widetilde h(x)=Af_0(x).
 \label{eq:nonlinear_side}
\end{equation}
The healthy base $f_0$ stays fixed, and a two-ReLU side MLP $g_\psi$ learns its nonlinear prefix. Both heads receive one shared $64$-vector. Projection preserves affine-consumer logits; the following experiment measures whether the added flexibility also retains useful learning.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_nonlinear_interface.pdf}
\caption{A compatible nonlinear channel adds trainable capacity. (a) The base stays fixed while the side learns, with $AP_\perp g_\psi=0$. (b) Equal-depth paths fuse into a dense width-$64+k$ MLP: the middle matrix is block diagonal and the final side block is projected. The base is embedded once, all dense zero blocks count toward storage and computation, and the output stays $64$-dimensional. Planes are schematic; parameter ratios and the validation screen are reported separately.}
\label{fig:nonlinear_interface}
\end{figure}

For base weights $(U_1,U_2,U_3)$ and side weights $(V_1,V_2,V_3)$, the fused matrices are
\begin{equation}
 \overline U_1=\begin{bmatrix}U_1\\V_1\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \overline U_2=\begin{bmatrix}U_2&0\\0&V_2\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \overline U_3=\begin{bmatrix}U_3&P_\perp V_3\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:nonlinear_fusion}
\end{equation}
Stacking the first two biases and using final bias $b_3+P_\perp c_3$ gives the same function with three affine layers and two ReLUs. The fused model embeds the base once and absorbs the separate projection. Its wider dense matrices still count all zero blocks: for side widths $16/32$, encoder parameter ratios are $1.301/1.623$ on SNR and $1.279/1.569$ on HAR, at native float64. Full-data runtime evaluation was contingent on passing the development screen. The side's final weight and bias start at zero; prefix weights copy the first $k$ healthy units and their $k\times k$ second-layer block.

The new plan was committed before numerical execution, after observing the earlier studies. Development uses seeds $500$--$502$, the original width-$64$ base, Adam $10^{-3}$, batch $256$, and the same stratified $20\%$ training reservation before model/head fitting. SNR uses $20\%$ of fitting primary labels, dose $1$, and $25+25$ healthy/continuation epochs. HAR uses a new $1\%$ fitting-label reconstruction regime, doses $1,10$, and $60+30$ epochs. Both test side widths $16,32$. All feature-encoder arithmetic is float64, with fp32 head parameters and float64 classification logits. These changes prevent pooling with earlier confirmation results.

Each candidate compares full-network D/J, unconstrained side UD/UJ, and constrained side TD/TJ, with detached/joint auxiliary gradients. Healthy base/head parameters and their Adam states are retained; new side Adam states start empty. The common parameter ordering, paired side initialization, fitting-label masks and future minibatches are checked. The declared score is the minimum of three mean validation gains (J--D, TJ--TD, TJ--D) and the mean TJ--J contrast plus $0.5$ point; ties prefer smaller width, then dose. A task enters ten-new-seed test confirmation only if its best score is strictly positive. No development test outcomes are computed.

\subsection{A Vision Boundary Screen}\label{app:vision_boundary}

The failure requires an update that changes the scale of the features; a mild
auxiliary on a pretrained vision backbone need not. We ran one prespecified
development screen and did not open the official test split. The model is an
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100 at $64\times64$ input, continued for
three epochs with SGD at $10^{-3}$ for the encoder and $10^{-2}$ for the heads,
momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$, batch $128$, and frozen
batch-normalization running statistics. Three branches cloned the same pretrained
model and shared minibatches: primary-only, and a rotation auxiliary at dose $1$
and dose $10$. A linear readout fitted on the healthy features reached $0.513$
validation accuracy. After continuation it did not degrade: the frozen readout
reached $0.598$ in the primary-only branch and $0.588$ under the rotation
auxiliary, while the corresponding current heads reached $0.616$ and $0.615$. The
auxiliary also gave no measurable primary benefit ($-0.0006$). The dose-$10$
branch produced a non-finite loss in its first epoch and was not retuned or
rerun. The prespecified failure criterion was a loss of at least five points, so
this screen is negative and no unlabeled repair is credited: the fixed
diagonal-statistics family stayed near $0.61$--$0.62$, and the paired-ridge
family was unstable across anchor budgets ($0.17$--$0.58$). We report the screen
as a boundary rather than a contradiction. Continuation training need not produce
the failure, and this run does not identify which property of the update
prevented it; where the update leaves the usable scale intact, the existing
readout keeps working, which is what the affine boundary predicts. Historical
CIFAR work in this project used a different protocol (from-scratch coarse/fine
supervision), and its reader-confirmation gate also failed; we do not merge those
reports with this screen.

\subsection{A Fixed-Corpus Unlabeled Consumer Check}\label{app:consumer_audit}

For a present checkpoint, we complement the variance audit with unlabeled disagreement against the healthy consumer. This uses the standard relative-loss inequality and exact finite-population sampling. It sits alongside unlabeled performance estimation~\citep{garg2022unlabeled} and update certificates~\citep{usama2026envelopes}, with reference predictions and an explicit abstention option.

For a fixed corpus of $N$ inputs, let $f_0$ be the healthy consumer, $f$ a fixed candidate, and $R_T$ the mean $0$--$1$ error on subset $T$. For every assignment of labels,
\begin{equation}
 R_T(f)-R_T(f_0)\le D_T(f,f_0),\qquad
 D_T=|T|^{-1}\sum_{i\in T}\mathbf1\{f(x_i)\ne f_0(x_i)\}.
 \label{eq:consumer_disagreement}
\end{equation}
The inequality follows pointwise: error can increase only where predictions change. It is sharp when the labels equal the healthy predictions. Conversely, changing predictions can improve accuracy, so large disagreement does not establish harm.

Uniformly sample $n$ monitor inputs without replacement, independently of the fixed candidate family. If $c$ predictions disagree on this monitor and the unknown full-corpus disagreement count is $K$, then $c$ has the hypergeometric distribution with parameters $(N,K,n)$. With family size $M$ and total tail probability $\delta$, define
\begin{equation}
 K^+(c)=\max\{K\in\{c,\ldots,N-n+c\}:F_{N,K,n}(c)\ge\delta/M\},\qquad
 U=\frac{K^+(c)-c}{N-n}.
 \label{eq:consumer_finite}
\end{equation}
Here $F$ is the exact hypergeometric CDF. Inverting its lower tail bounds the full-corpus disagreement count, hence $D_T\le U$ on the unmonitored remainder. For each candidate the failure probability is at most $\delta/M$; a union bound gives simultaneous coverage for all $M$ candidates, allowing selection from that fixed family. This random-partition statement does not require independent adjacent HAR windows. It covers the fixed corpus, rather than a new population or future optimizer trajectory. The bound concerns classification error; it does not certify NLL, ECE, or conformal coverage. Monitor-adaptive encoder training or unconstrained construction of new repair candidates would require a different analysis.

We predeclare $n=1{,}024$, $\delta=0.05$, and budgets $0.05$ and $0.10$ for relative loss increase. The bank contains all 300 endpoints from the 30 earlier paired runs plus 35 CNN endpoints, each evaluated with the same nine fixed repair arms: $M=\ConsumerFamily$. All maps use the full fitting feature set; a restricted anchor budget would need its own evaluation. A seeded uniform monitor partition is shared across candidates in a run, and old/new reference predictions on those monitor inputs are required. Healthy reference predictions can be cached for a fixed corpus or obtained from the retained healthy encoder; this access is additional to paired training anchors for richer repairs. The first repair in the predeclared hierarchy satisfying $U\le\varepsilon$ is selected, or the audit abstains. Monitor labels never enter this choice. Candidate remainder accuracy and loss increase are scored only after choices are fixed; archived healthy/frozen metrics are also checked independently for reproducibility.

\section{Anti-Collapse Regularizers at Matched Doses}\label{app:reg}

Table~\ref{tab:reg} evaluates two regularizer families at the doses and seeds of Table~\ref{tab:conflict}: VICReg's variance hinge plus off-diagonal covariance penalty, and Barlow Twins' redundancy-reduction penalty. Each is applied to the shared representation with weight $\lambda$. VICReg's invariance term is omitted because this experiment has no augmented-view pair. All rows use the same joint-training baseline; these are adaptations of the regularizers to this setting, not evaluations of their complete original learning pipelines.

Regularization can clear the operational collapse threshold without restoring the single-task reference or improving the task. In the coarse grid, VICReg first avoids collapse in all seeds at $\lambda=10$ for HAR, $300$ for battery, and $10000$ for RadioML. Barlow Twins does not do so at any tested weight.

On HAR, VICReg scatter stays near $0.36$ from $\lambda=10$ to $10^4$, while accuracy falls from $0.944$ to $0.821$. At the lowest clearing weight, accuracy is close to joint training ($0.945$) and detachment. On battery, scatter increases through the reference level, but the best tested RMSE is $0.339$ at $\lambda=300$ (relative scatter $0.59$), compared with $0.297$ for detachment. Larger weights worsen RMSE to $0.390$ at $10^3$ and $0.423$ at $10^4$; detachment is better in nine of ten seeds. On RadioML, $\lambda=10000$ gives zero collapse and scatter $1.29$, but accuracy drops to $0.215$, below both collapsed joint training ($0.256$) and detachment ($0.341$). These cases separate the scatter criterion from predictive utility.

The Barlow Twins adaptation yields absolute scatter as low as $0.011$, $0.012$, and $0.013$ for HAR, battery, and RadioML, respectively, or factors of $172$, $6$, and $24$ below their references. It fails to clear the collapse threshold throughout the tested grid, with HAR accuracy reaching approximately $0.34$--$0.44$ and RadioML approximately $0.11$ in the low-accuracy configurations.

Additional ten-seed sweeps use $\lambda\in\{2,5,20,50,200,500\}$ on HAR and $\lambda\in\{200,500,700,1500,2000,5000\}$ on battery. The recorded battery transition is bracketed by $150$ and $200$, and the lowest measured battery RMSE remains $0.339$ at $\lambda=300$, above detachment. HAR scatter remains near $0.36$ above its crossing. RadioML was not refined: the sampled collapse rate is $1.0$ at $1000$, $0.10$ at $3000$, and $0.0$ at $10000$. The experiments therefore locate an operational transition over a finite grid rather than a precise critical weight.
