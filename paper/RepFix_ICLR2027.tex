\title{Gradient Toxicity: When Auxiliary Heads Collapse the Shared Encoder}

\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Auxiliary objectives are assumed to help a shared encoder. We identify a failure mode in which they destroy it, and which gradient-conflict methods cannot see because the offending gradients are \emph{aligned} rather than opposed: any auxiliary head with a \emph{constant attractor} --- a loss whose minimum a constant representation attains --- contributes a gradient along a single variance-degenerating direction, and above a measurable dose the shared representation collapses. An exact parameter-space identity separates auxiliary contraction from primary restoration, and conditional scalar and spectral models distinguish loss of a stable branch, contraction of individual modes, and full collapse; for a specified positive semidefinite operator we prove the minimum deletion rank that bounds its compressed spectrum. A frozen gradient probe provides a practical diagnostic: on UCI-HAR its inverse, $9.1\times10^{-3}$, lies inside the measured transition interval $[0.001,0.01]$. At the reported high-dose settings in four scientific domains, calibrated filtering reduces the ten-seed operational collapse rate from $10/10$ to $0/10$, with boundary-dose failures reported separately. Real auxiliary heads expose three limits that make the diagnostic actionable. Task error can improve as representation amplitude shrinks --- on NYUv2 depth error \emph{improves} under collapse --- and representation PCA can require excessive deletion, while a capped probe cannot establish spectral optimality. On a corrected cell-disjoint MATR protocol, detachment improves SOH RMSE from $5.57$ to $2.10$ and observed-RUL MAE from $81.5$ to $46.7$, so protection and task accuracy are not in tension once right-censoring and checkpoint selection are handled correctly. For HAR reconstruction, an auxiliary-gradient basis reduces mean rank among the four gate-open seeds from $62.0$ to $30.75$ at similar aggregate accuracy. Together these results connect measurable contraction to interventions while separating operator guarantees from empirical calibration.

\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Multi-task learning (MTL) rests on one working assumption: auxiliary tasks supply gradients that steer a shared encoder toward more generalizable representations. PCGrad~\citep{yu2020pcgrad}, FAMO~\citep{liu2023famo}, and CAGrad~\citep{liu2021cagrad} handle the case in which it fails through \emph{conflict} --- gradients pushing in different directions --- and all presuppose that each task contributes useful directional information.

The assumption can also fail in a way that conflict resolution cannot detect: auxiliary gradients may all point in the \emph{same} direction, and that direction may lead to a degenerate fixed point. \texttt{.detach()} is the folk remedy, but it discards the auxiliary signal wholesale: the question is \emph{which} projection to remove, and stop-gradient removes all of them.

We first met this failure in multi-task financial ranking, where a model with distributional, reconstruction and contrastive heads developed near-constant out-of-sample predictions that further training did not recover. The observation motivates examining representation variation alongside task loss. An auxiliary objective with a constant-representation minimum can encourage contraction, but the existence of that minimum alone does not determine its gradient away from the minimum. For a centered quadratic variance penalty, contraction follows directly; reconstruction with varying targets generally has no such minimum. We therefore measure each head's effect, and call a positive auxiliary contribution to instantaneous variance decrease \emph{gradient toxicity}, with the resulting \emph{representational collapse}. It is categorically distinct from conflict: agreement or near-orthogonality between task gradients does not determine their projection onto the parameter-space variance gradient, and toxic gradients from different heads are \emph{aligned} on a single variance-degenerating axis.

We investigate when auxiliary gradients contract useful representations, which diagnostics reveal the effect, and what filtering can preserve. The theoretical contribution distinguishes an exact signed variance identity, a conditional scalar transition model, and a minimum-rank PSD compression result. Their scopes differ: a practical PCA probe does not automatically inherit a Hessian theorem. Experiments compare full detachment with selective filters, identify basis sensitivity for real reconstruction heads, and examine battery task error alongside representation and prediction variation.

Our contributions are three. First, we identify gradient toxicity as a failure mode orthogonal to conflict --- auxiliary gradients that \emph{agree} while contracting a shared representation --- and give an exact parameter-space variance identity that separates the contraction from primary restoration, with the additional assumptions under which scalar and spectral models follow. Second, we characterize minimum-rank deletion for a specified PSD compression and derive a residual-energy criterion for choosing a reconstruction-gradient basis. Third, controlled experiments test the diagnostic across domains and encoders, while real-head experiments identify where calibration succeeds or fails, and we report the three findings that bound it: the primary metric can improve under collapse, a principal-basis surrogate can over-delete, and probe depth alone does not make the rank rule optimal. The corrected battery evaluation and matched attenuation baseline distinguish improved task accuracy, representation amplitude, and selective deletion: none is used as a proxy for the others.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Multi-task optimization~\citep{caruana1997multitask} has concentrated on \emph{negative transfer between tasks}. PCGrad projects conflicting gradients~\citep{yu2020pcgrad}, CAGrad optimizes a worst-case combination~\citep{liu2021cagrad}, Nash-MTL casts combination as a bargaining game~\citep{navon2022multi}, and FAMO balances losses via first-order per-task progress~\citep{liu2023famo}; multi-objective formulations target Pareto-stationary points~\citep{sener2018multi}; uncertainty weighting~\citep{kendall2018multi}, GradNorm~\citep{chen2018gradnorm}, IMTL~\citep{liu2021imtl}, and Aligned-MTL~\citep{senushkin2023aligned,wang2021gradient} reweight or rotate gradients, a family still active~\citep{limarenko2026gcond,zhou2025imgrad}; and task-grouping~\citep{standley2020tasks} and dense-prediction~\citep{vandenhende2021multi} studies map when co-training helps. These methods motivate a complementary diagnostic: how a proposed update changes representation variance. Gradient alignment alone does not determine this change (Section~\ref{sec:conflict_ablations}).

Representation collapse and its stop-gradient remedy form a second line of work; we use \emph{collapse} for a degenerate, variance-vanishing representation, not the benign terminal geometry of \emph{neural collapse}~\citep{papyan2020prevalence,wang2026neuralcollapse}, a property of converged classifiers rather than a failure. The \texttt{.detach()} trick appears across self-supervised learning (BYOL~\citep{grill2020bootstrap}, SimSiam~\citep{chen2021simsiam}, DINO~\citep{caron2021emerging}), where it prevents collapse, while non-contrastive objectives counteract the same failure by explicit variance/covariance regularization (Barlow Twins~\citep{zbontar2021barlow}, VICReg~\citep{bardes2022vicreg}, whitening~\citep{ermolov2021whitening}); related theory traces collapse to singular-value dynamics~\citep{jing2022understanding,dong2021attention,kim2025taxonomy}, and rank-based scores track it empirically~\citep{garrido2023rankme}. Auxiliary tasks are selected and weighted to \emph{improve} the shared representation~\citep{navon2021auxiliary,shin2024dgr}, or their negative transfer is mitigated directly~\citep{jiang2023forkmerge}. We investigate auxiliary-gradient filtering alongside these approaches, separating exact variance dynamics from conditional models and empirical projected-gradient diagnostics.

Our validation domains come from time-series sensing and energy. Human activity recognition (UCI-HAR~\citep{ucihar}), radio-frequency modulation identification (RadioML~\citep{radioml}), and battery state-of-health prediction (NASA PCoE~\citep{nasa_battery}) are canonical multi-task time-series problems where auxiliary tasks routinely augment the primary encoder~\citep{zhang2021survey}. Their scales, dimensionalities, and objectives differ, so we treat them as independent validation domains and evaluate on domain-independent quantities (Section~\ref{sec:unified_protocol}).

Battery health is an active MTL application: data-driven models predict cycle life and SOH directly~\citep{severson2019data,attia2020closed,pradhan2022soh}, with loss weighting balancing the heads. We examine how auxiliary reconstruction and distributional objectives affect the encoder, rather than assuming their toxicity from their names. Section~\ref{sec:battery} evaluates SOH and RUL separately, with right-censoring and validation-based checkpoint selection.

Table~\ref{tab:related} summarizes the positioning. Subspace manipulation is established: ATTITTUD decomposes auxiliary updates by primary-task influence~\citep{dery2021attittud}, and GradOPS projects against task-gradient spans~\citep{zhu2025gradops}. Our target is constant-attractor contraction, which need not coincide with conflict. The distinction requires a matched failure diagnosis and a stated deletion objective; projection alone is not the contribution.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

We study multi-task representation learning with a shared encoder $E_\theta:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^m$, a primary head $f_p$ with loss $L_p$, and $K$ auxiliary heads with losses $\{L_a^{(k)}\}$. The training objective is
\begin{equation}
\min_\theta\;\; \mathbb{E}_b\Big[\, L_p\big(f_p(E_\theta(x))\big) \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_k\, L_a^{(k)}\big(h_a^{(k)}(E_\theta(x))\big) \Big],
\label{eq:mtl_obj}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_k\ge0$ controls auxiliary strength, $h_a^{(k)}$ are auxiliary heads, and the batch expectation $\mathbb{E}_b$ is over training samples. Throughout, $h=E_\theta(x)$ denotes the shared representation and $g_p=\nabla_h L_p$, $g_a=\nabla_h L_a$ the primary and auxiliary gradients with respect to $h$.

We use MLPs in the unified protocol and other encoders in the application studies. The variance identity is general; the dynamical reductions below require their own explicit assumptions. One useful structural property of the synthetic auxiliary is:

\begin{definition}[Degenerate minimizer]\label{def:degmin}
A loss has a degenerate minimizer if some constant representation attains its minimum. Stationarity is necessary for a differentiable interior minimum, but is not sufficient. Variance shrinkage has such minima. Reconstruction with varying targets generally does not: a constant code need not even be stationary for a fixed decoder. The behavior of a trained reconstruction head must therefore be measured, rather than inferred from its name.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Projected-gradient diagnostic]\label{def:toxicity}
For a nonconstant batch representation let $u_c=h-\mathbb E_b[h]$ and $\Pi_c g=\langle g,u_c\rangle u_c/\|u_c\|^2$. We retain the historical diagnostic
\begin{equation}
\rho=\|\Pi_cg_a\|/\|g_p\|,\qquad \|g_p\|>0.
\label{eq:order}
\end{equation}
It measures projected magnitude, not signed contraction: $g_a$ and $-g_a$ have the same $\rho$. Implemented probes average regularized per-example ratios, which need not equal this ratio of batch norms. Neither statistic is automatically a dynamical order parameter.
\end{definition}

An exact alternative uses parameters. On a fixed batch of size $B$, set $V(\theta)=\|C_BH(\theta)\|_F^2/(2B)$, where $C_B=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/B$ centers \emph{examples}. Write $G_p=\nabla_\theta L_p$, $G_a=\nabla_\theta L_a$ and $v=\nabla_\theta V$. Gradient flow satisfies
\begin{equation}
\dot V=r-\alpha c,\qquad r=-v^\top G_p,\quad c=v^\top G_a.
\label{eq:signed_variance}
\end{equation}
This chain-rule identity includes the encoder Jacobian. Only $c>0$ denotes auxiliary contraction; for $r,c>0$, $\alpha c/r>1$ is equivalent to instantaneous variance decrease. It does not establish eventual collapse. Gradient conflict, $G_p^\top G_a<0$, is a different condition.

% 4. GRADIENT TOXICITY THEORY
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Gradient Toxicity Theory}\label{sec:theory}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional scalar collapse transition]\label{thm:transition}
Assume an independently justified scalar reduction
\begin{equation}
\dot\sigma=-\alpha\kappa\sigma+b(\sigma),\qquad\kappa>0,
\label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
where $b(0)=0$. Let $f(\sigma)=b(\sigma)/\sigma$ on $(0,\infty)$ tend to zero at both ends, increase strictly to a unique nondegenerate maximum $\lambda_\star=f(\sigma_\star)>0$, and decrease strictly afterwards. Then $\alpha_c=\lambda_\star/\kappa$. For $0<\alpha<\alpha_c$ there are two positive equilibria, the smaller unstable and the larger stable; initial values below the smaller one still collapse. At $\alpha_c$ the equilibria coalesce in a saddle-node; for $\alpha>\alpha_c$, every positive trajectory tends to zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vector field is $\sigma(f(\sigma)-\alpha\kappa)$. The crossings and their stability follow from monotonicity; the nonzero second derivative gives the nondegenerate fold. Above the maximum the field is strictly negative for every $\sigma>0$, whose only possible limiting equilibrium is zero.
\end{proof}
The scalar assumptions do not follow from a constant minimizer alone. In particular, identifying $\kappa/\lambda_\star$ with the measured $\rho$ requires an additional calibration identity. We test the probe empirically rather than asserting this identity for arbitrary heads or optimizers.

\begin{lemma}[Departure from a primary optimum]\label{lem:irreversible}
At $G_p=0$, a nonzero $G_a$ makes the joint gradient nonzero for $\alpha>0$. This establishes departure from the primary optimum, not collapse or irreversibility.
\end{lemma}
For example, $L_p(h)=(h-1)^2/2$ and $L_a(h)=h^2/2$ have joint optimum $h=1/(1+\alpha)>0$ at every finite dose. Constant predictions likewise need not have zero parameter gradients (Proposition~\ref{prop:gradient_vanishing}). These distinctions prevent a large norm ratio near a stationary point from being interpreted as a collapse guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-dose attenuation]\label{cor:stf}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:transition}, multiplying the auxiliary term by a constant $q\in(0,1]$ replaces the effective dose by $q\alpha$ and the threshold by $\alpha_c/q$. Suitable positive attenuation can retain the positive equilibrium at a fixed dose. Exact deletion is sufficient to remove that contraction term, but is not necessary for fixed-dose protection.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional capacity scaling]\label{thm:capacity}
If the scalar restoration maximum scales as $m^a$ and contraction as $m^b m_\Pi^c$, with $m_\Pi\asymp m^\beta$, then
\begin{equation}
\alpha_c(m)\asymp m^\gamma,\qquad\gamma=a-b-c\beta.
\label{eq:capacity_law}
\end{equation}
The restricted law $\gamma=\gamma_0(1-\beta)$ requires $a-b=c=\gamma_0$. Fixed rank alone implies neither $\gamma>0$ nor $\gamma_0=1$.
\end{theorem}
This follows by dividing restoration by contraction. Width parametrization and loss normalization affect these rates~\citep{yang2021feature}. Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_dilution} separates the assumptions from measured exponents; the original random generator couples effective rank and curvature, so its fits cannot identify both effects.

\begin{theorem}[First modal contraction versus full linear collapse]\label{thm:spectral}
For the explicitly linear symmetric model $\dot z=(R-\alpha A)z$, assume $R\succ0$ and $A\succeq0$ in the same coordinates. Along an eigenvector of $R-\alpha A$ with eigenvalue $\nu_j$, the modal variance obeys
\begin{equation}
\dot\varsigma_j^2=2\nu_j\varsigma_j^2.
\label{eq:modal}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
T=R^{-1/2}AR^{-1/2},\qquad \rho_\star=\lambda_{\max}(T).
\label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
Some mode contracts iff $\alpha\rho_\star>1$. Every mode contracts exponentially iff $\alpha\lambda_{\min}(T)>1$. Equality leaves a neutral mode.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$R-\alpha A=R^{1/2}(I-\alpha T)R^{1/2}$ has the same inertia as $I-\alpha T$ by congruence. Since the generator is symmetric, its eigenvalue signs determine modal growth or decay.
\end{proof}
The two thresholds differ: $R=I$, $A=\mathrm{diag}(2,1/2)$ and $\alpha=1$ contract one mode while expanding the other. A covariance eigenvector need not be a dynamical eigenvector. Nor is this linear model a derivation of the nonlinear saddle-node model. Aligned and orthogonal rank-one operators obey the elementary composition rule in Corollary~\ref{cor:additive}, with the threshold referring to \emph{first modal contraction}.

For a specified PSD operator, Theorem~\ref{thm:minimalrank} gives an exact minimum deletion rank for a symmetric compression. It does not establish that a representation-PCA residual is that operator's spectrum. Finite-sample concentration (Proposition~\ref{prop:calibration}) controls estimation of a specified statistic, not this modeling gap or future encoder drift.

\section{Spectral Toxicity Filtering}\label{sec:stf}

Motivated by projected-gradient diagnostics, consider the idealized backward filter
\begin{equation}
F_\beta[g_a]=g_a-\Pi_cg_a\,w_\beta(\rho),\qquad w_\beta(\rho)=\mathrm{sig}(\beta(\alpha\rho-1)).
\label{eq:stf}
\end{equation}
The encoder update uses $J_\theta^\top(g_p+\alpha F_\beta[g_a])$. Full detachment (\textsc{stf-0}) removes all auxiliary encoder gradients; \textsc{stf-hard} thresholds the projection; \textsc{stf-soft} attenuates it smoothly. For finite temperature, the sigmoid does not exactly annihilate the projection and supplies no general safety guarantee.

Within a fixed scalar model, attenuation by $1-\lambda$ gives threshold $\alpha_c/(1-\lambda)$. At a fixed operating point, the least attenuation meeting $(1-\lambda)\alpha\rho_\star\le1$ is $\max(0,1-1/(\alpha\rho_\star))$, not a bang-bang gate. Hard deletion is one intervention choice; positive scalar weighting is a necessary matched baseline. General one-sided backward filters cannot be assigned dynamical thresholds from their operator norms alone.

The historical implementations differ from this idealized backward map. They pass a transformed representation to the auxiliary head: $\widetilde H=H-wC_BHP$, where $C_B$ centers examples and $P$ is a feature projector; the primary head still sees $H$. For fixed $w,P$, the auxiliary encoder gradient is $G_a(\widetilde H)-wC_BG_a(\widetilde H)P$, not a projection of $G_a(H)$. The uncalibrated hard/soft gates use a covariance spectral ratio; the calibrated gate below uses a gradient ratio. Radial filtering removes centered variation when open and preserves the batch mean, whereas \textsc{stf-0} uses \texttt{detach()} and preserves auxiliary forward values. A separate backward-only pilot is reported in the reconstruction-basis analysis below. These distinctions matter when comparing deletion to reweighting.

\subsection{Calibrated Spectral Filtering: \textsc{stfcal}}\label{sec:stfcal}

The released \textsc{stfcal} protocol fixes a gate from a detached warm-up anchor. Initialization and later checkpoints yield very different $\hat\rho$ even in healthy runs (Appendix~\ref{app:online}), motivating a fixed read time. This is an operational calibration choice rather than a theorem that an online diagnostic cannot work.

During warm-up only the primary loss updates the encoder; auxiliary heads may still train on detached features. A probe yields $\hat\rho$ and residual diagnostics $\hat\rho_{\mathrm{res}}[0..R]$, defining
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{gate}=\mathbf1[\alpha\hat\rho\ge1],\qquad r=\min\{r\le R:\alpha\hat\rho_{\mathrm{res}}[r]<1\}.
\label{eq:calrule}
\end{equation}
The released rank arm freezes the leading representation principal directions. If no tested rank meets the residual threshold, it uses the rank cap, which is not a certificate of safety; the implementation also clamps an active selected rank to at least one. The separate \textsc{stfcal} arm removes each example's centered radial component when the gate opens, independently of the rank search. Algorithm~\ref{alg:stfcal} states these forward maps. Warm-up length, probe size, basis and rank cap remain protocol choices. A frozen basis may become stale, and neither the ratio threshold nor latching supplies a trajectory-wide guarantee.

The rule is minimal only among the tested nested residuals for the specified statistic. Theorem~\ref{thm:minimalrank} instead concerns eigenvalues of a specified PSD compression; transferring it to this implementation requires a separate identification argument. Conditional concentration can certify the frozen statistic under independent bounded sampling, but cannot turn representation PCA into an auxiliary Hessian basis or certify the trained network.

On UCI-HAR at $m=64$ a $4$-epoch detached warm-up followed by one probe gives $\hat\rho=110.2$ (mean over $10$ seeds; range $42$--$296$), hence a predicted $\alpha_c=9.1\times10^{-3}$, inside the measured interval $\alpha_c\in[0.001,0.01]$ (Section~\ref{sec:har}) --- the probe precedes any auxiliary gradient.
Ten seeds per cell replicate the dose--response across five domains and two encoders: joint collapses $10/10$ at supercritical $\alpha$ while \textsc{stfcal} collapses $0/10$ with the gate open in every seed, and on battery it beats joint on SOH-RMSE ($0.298$ vs.\ $0.348$). The protection transfers to a Transformer encoder at RadioML $\alpha{=}3000$, where joint collapses $10/10$ to accuracy $0.156$ and \textsc{stfcal} holds $0/10$ at scatter $0.970$. CelebA~\citep{liu2015celeba} (Smiling head) is the most toxic configuration measured ($\hat\rho=1602$--$3585$): at $\alpha{=}100$ \textsc{stfcal} protects fully ($0/5$) while joint collapses $5/5$. NYUv2 dense perception (Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}) adds the metric-silent regime: the calibrated $\alpha_c=0.50$ brackets the observed transition, the gate opens exactly in the five seeds with $\hat\rho>1/\alpha$ (Figure~\ref{fig:unified}f), and joint depth RMSE \emph{improves} under collapse ($1.33\to1.09$), so the task metric alone does not reveal representation shrinkage. Per-domain scatter values are in Appendix~\ref{app:moved}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Unified Protocol}\label{sec:unified_protocol}

The controlled comparisons share the gradient diagnostic and relative-scatter criterion (Algorithm~\ref{alg:stf}); architecture and batch details are stated per experiment. The default tabular/time-series encoder is a 3-layer MLP of width $m$; the primary task is domain-specific (accuracy or RMSE); and the toxic auxiliary task is the \emph{variance-shrinkage toxifier} $L_{tox}=\mathrm{Var}_b[W_k h]$ over $K=3$ seeded random projections, which realizes the constant attractor of Definition~\ref{def:degmin}. Training uses Adam ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$, batch $256$) with bf16 autocast on an NVIDIA A100 80\,GB and $5$--$10$ seeds per cell, with the relative-collapse criterion $\mathcal{S}_h<0.3\,\mathcal{S}_{single}$ on the cross-sample scatter
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_h \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{j}\big[\mathrm{std}_b( h^{(b)}_{j} - \mathbb{E}_b[h^{(b)}_{j}] )\big],
\label{eq:cross_sample_scatter}
\end{equation}
anchored per seed to its own single-task ceiling. The toxifier is a controlled variance objective, but its combined rank and normalization must be audited across widths; Section~\ref{sec:bmtl} repeats the measurements with production heads. Domain-independent metrics are the \emph{collapse rate}, the \emph{recovery rate}, the order parameter $\rho$, and the relative scatter $\mathcal{S}_h/\mathcal{S}_{single}$; domain characteristics and sample counts are in Table~\ref{tab:domains} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). Over per-seed values ($n{=}10$ per domain), the \textsc{joint}-vs-\textsc{stfcal} scatter increase is significant at $p<0.01$ in every domain (Wilcoxon; $|d_z|>20$) and battery SOH-RMSE improves by $0.050$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.033, 0.067]$, $d_z=1.8$); the four scatter comparisons and one battery SOH comparison survive Holm correction as a five-test family (\texttt{paired\_stats.py} recomputes both tests from the shipped per-seed records). Every $\pm$ denotes population standard deviation across the stated seeds or cells; confidence intervals are identified separately. Fold-cell summaries share data and are not 100 independent financial replicates.

\subsection{Cross-domain validation}\label{sec:validation}

Five domains are run under one protocol, with domain-specific primary tasks and auxiliary heads: A-share ranking (Appendix~\ref{app:finance})\label{sec:finance}; UCI-HAR\label{sec:har}; RadioML2016.10A\label{sec:radioml}; NASA/MATR battery state-of-health\label{sec:battery}; and NYUv2 dense perception\label{sec:nyu} (Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}), with CelebA as a sixth-instance toxicity check. At the high-dose controlled settings reported here, \textsc{joint} meets the operational collapse criterion and calibrated filtering restores scatter (collapse rate $1.0\to0.0$); this does not cover every method or boundary dose; the discovery domain is scored on its own primary metric, rank-IC, where the gate keeps the auxiliary signal that hard detachment discards. The repair is complete where collapse is primary-metric-visible: RadioML accuracy returns from the chance level $0.091$ to $0.33$ and battery log-SOH RMSE from $0.611$ to $0.305$ (Figure~\ref{fig:battery_repair}; Table~\ref{tab:unified_repair}).

The domains also differ in \emph{how visible} the collapse is to the primary metric. On RadioML and battery the metric itself collapses; on HAR the classifier still reaches $\approx0.95$ accuracy at relative scatter $0.05$; and on MATR an encoder with strongly reduced amplitude (relative scatter $0.0052$) can score \emph{better} SOH-RMSE than detachment ($0.82$ vs.\ $1.51$), in this particular protocol; the metric alone does not identify the cause. Small representation amplitude need not mean constant predictions, especially with a trainable head. Task error, prediction variance and representation covariance should therefore be examined together.

\subsection{BatteryMTL: A Flagship Application for Joint SOH and RUL Prediction}\label{sec:bmtl}

The battery domain is also the natural setting for a \emph{production-style} application: a multi-task model over a strong temporal encoder that jointly predicts state-of-health (SOH) and remaining useful life (RUL), with exactly the auxiliary heads such systems routinely attach --- the configuration the unified protocol diagnoses.

A \textsc{TSMixer}~\citep{ekambaram2023tsmixer} block plus a gated state-space block~\citep{gu2022s4,gu2023mamba} map each cell's per-cycle window to $h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, which feeds a SOH head ($L_2$), a RUL head ($L_1$), and auxiliary reconstruction/distributional heads whose effect on the encoder is tested empirically (Appendix~\ref{app:moved}).

RUL targets are right-censored. Training uses absolute error for observed lifetimes and a one-sided hinge for lower bounds. The historical driver nevertheless computes its reported RUL MAE over \emph{all} evaluation samples, including censored lower bounds; it is not uncensored lifetime MAE. It also selects every checkpoint by evaluation SOH, including the RUL-only reference. Those RUL columns are retained as legacy scores, not valid lifetime-error ceilings. The revised validation protocol reports observed-only MAE and censored shortfall separately, after validation-based checkpoint selection.

Under the corrected protocol, detachment improves both MATR tasks (Table~\ref{tab:bmtl_corrected}): the paired mean difference from joint is $-3.47$ SOH percentage points (95\% $t$ interval $[-4.20,-2.74]$) and $-34.8$ observed-RUL cycles ($[-57.8,-11.8]$). This closes $70\%$ of the SOH gap to the SOH-only reference. The intervals describe ten initializations on one fixed cell split, not uncertainty over new cell populations. Correcting several protocol choices together does not identify censoring as the cause of the earlier ordering. Selective filtering does not beat detachment here, and the rank arm has very large covariance trace but low effective rank. Neither the capped search nor this behavior proves spectral saturation. On NASA, detachment also improves SOH, but its observed-RUL difference is inconclusive (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\subsection{Comparison with Conflict-Resolution Methods}\label{sec:conflict_ablations}

Pairwise gradient conflict is not equivalent to variance contraction, so we compare conflict methods empirically at the same doses. A direct measurement on the four real heads confirms it: over ten seeds $|\cos(g_p,g_a)|\le0.06$ with a sign-balanced dot product, while on the SNR head --- the one that collapses --- $88\%$ of $\|g_a\|$ lies on the single collapse axis $u_c$ (benign heads: $1$--$14\%$). \section{Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Capacity Law, Diagnostics, and Real Auxiliary Heads}\label{sec:capacity_law}\label{sec:diagnosis}\label{sec:real_aux}\label{sec:negatives}

We estimate empirical half-height crossings and fit $\log\alpha_c=\gamma\log m+c$. Historical four-width slopes are $2.43$ for the fixed-per-head-rank schedule, $0.66$ for HAR, $0.10$ for battery, and $0.02$ for RadioML; nine widths give $0.38$ for HAR and $-0.06$ for battery. These are descriptive crossing exponents, not independently identified restoration or toxic-rank exponents. In particular, inferring $\beta$ from these slopes would assume the rank-dilution law being tested. An exact-rank orthonormal control in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} reproduces that confound, so $\gamma_0$ is left without a physical value. Two measurements bound what can be claimed. Fitting the whole synthetic family jointly, where the generator pivots at the width whose toxic rank is common to every $\beta$, gives $\gamma_0=2.50$ with $95\%$ confidence interval $[1.94,3.06]$ --- excluding zero under both anchors and with or without the widest cell, under those specified cell-level fits. Shared seeds and identical pivot curves preclude interpreting this as independent-sample evidence for a universal law. The unconstrained fit does not reject the separable form $\gamma=\gamma_0(1-\beta)$: the implied dilution factor is $0.78$--$1.03$ against the predicted $1.00$. Repeating width sweeps at different fixed per-head ranks, however, does not reproduce a single exponent --- the measured slopes are $-0.31$, $+0.45$ and $+3.02$ at $r=4,8,16$ --- and the point estimate of $\gamma_0$ moves over $[1.35,2.87]$ as the width set changes. We therefore report the interval and the form of the law, not a value.

Task metrics can be insensitive to substantial changes in representation scatter. We therefore report them separately and distinguish the projected-norm diagnostic from signed contraction. Agreement between a probe and an empirical crossing at one width does not establish a shared width exponent, and prediction normalization supplies no general recovery theorem.

The four real-head comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:real_aux} show heterogeneous outcomes. The two reconstruction heads do not cross the reported collapse threshold at the tested doses, while high-dose SNR regression does. Full detachment avoids that empirical collapse criterion in all ten seeds; the tested selective filters do not consistently recover accuracy. This establishes a limitation of those implementations, not the impossibility of other selective filters. At RadioML dose $1$ the probe can also remain closed despite measured joint collapse, a false negative requiring further diagnosis. Basis drift and inadequate probe depth are hypotheses to test rather than conclusions supplied by the subspace bound, and we tested the second on the flagship domain: raising the calibrated probe from $3$ to $32$ directions leaves the rank arm at $5.26$ against plain summation's $5.24$, while the rank-free fallback branch reaches $1.77$. A cap of 32 is still below the feature width, so this failure does not rule out inadequate depth; it establishes that this particular increase is insufficient.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Gradient toxicity is a failure mode that conflict resolution is not designed to see: the toxic gradients agree with each other, and their agreement is what contracts the representation. The signed parameter-space variance identity locates it, conditional scalar and PSD models separate local contraction from full collapse and characterize minimum-rank spectral deletion, and a frozen gradient probe calibrated on one healthy batch identifies useful intervention regimes, while the real-head results establish the limits of that transfer. On MATR, detachment improves both SOH and observed RUL under a corrected validation protocol, without establishing representation recovery. On HAR reconstruction, an auxiliary-gradient basis uses substantially fewer directions than representation PCA at similar accuracy. Warm-up sensitivity, capped-rank failures and competitive scalar attenuation remain essential controls. What the theory guarantees stays scoped to its stated assumptions; the practical result is a diagnostic and a tested family of interventions, with explicit boundaries between empirical performance and operator-level guarantees.

\section{Origin of the Discovery: Multi-Task Financial Ranking}\label{app:finance}

Multi-task equity selection was the setting in which the failure mode was first observed. A production model coupled a pairwise-rank primary head with auxiliary distributional (MDN~\citep{bishop1994mixture}), reconstruction (AE), and view-contrastive (SCARF-style~\citep{bahri2022scarf}) heads over a shared encoder of A-share factors. After deployment, without any change to the training objective, the model began to output a near-constant ranking; cross-sectional predicted spreads degraded by $\sim$93\% against its validation history, while each individual objective still reported low training loss. This motivates examining representation and prediction variation alongside scalar objectives; the observation alone does not identify a constant attractor or a vanishing gradient.

The financial application uses its own walk-forward model and real distributional, reconstruction and contrastive objectives. The shipped R5fin records have \texttt{fixed\_aux=0}, so Table~\ref{tab:finance_repair} is not a replication of the controlled variance toxifier. Across ten folds and ten initialization seeds, mean OOS rank-IC is $0.060$ for joint, $0.036$ for detachment, $0.063$ for the hard gate and $0.056$ for the soft gate. Averaging seeds within each fold gives paired differences from joint of $-0.0237$, $+0.0029$ and $-0.0042$, respectively. The corresponding two-sided Wilcoxon $p$ values are $0.037$, $0.432$ and $0.695$; none survives Holm correction over these three comparisons. Temporal folds can still be dependent, so this is a sensitivity analysis, not an independent-population test. These results suggest a cost of indiscriminate detachment in this application, but neither prove retention of a particular useful subspace nor validate the scalar corollary. The script \texttt{finance\_fold\_audit.py} recomputes the comparisons from all 40 source files.

Measured initialization spectra give $\rho=0.74\pm0.19$ (12 seeds, synthetic testbed), whence the predicted critical weight $\alpha_c=\langle 1/\rho\rangle=1.45\pm0.37$, consistent with the observed onset location; the same $\rho$ is reproduced in every scientific domain via the projection estimate of Definition~\ref{def:toxicity}.

\section{Proofs and Additional Analysis}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Statements deferred from the main text}
For readability, the following statements are made in the main text only in prose; we collect their
formal form here. Numbering is shared with the main text.

\begin{proposition}[Sufficient conditions for gradient vanishing]\label{prop:gradient_vanishing}
For a differentiable prediction map with Jacobian $J$ and output loss gradient $\ell$, the parameter gradient vanishes iff $J^\top\ell=0$. Sufficient conditions are $\ell=0$, or identical Jacobian rows together with $\mathbf1^\top\ell=0$. Equal prediction values alone imply neither condition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the chain rule. For a counterexample to the stronger assertion, $\hat y_i=\theta x_i$ is constant at $\theta=0$ but has Jacobian rows $x_i$. The pairwise logistic loss $\log(1+e^{-(\hat y_1-\hat y_2)})$ has output gradient $(-1/2,1/2)$ at a tie, so its parameter gradient is $(x_2-x_1)/2$, generally nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scope of positive attenuation]\label{prop:minimality}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:transition}, any fixed positive scalar multiplier $q$ leaves a finite threshold $\alpha_c/q$, but can prevent a transition at a given dose. A dose-dependent positive multiplier can even keep the effective dose subcritical for all $\alpha$: for $0<\epsilon<1$, choose $q(\alpha)=(1-\epsilon)/(1+\alpha/\alpha_c)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Replace $\alpha$ by $q\alpha$. For the stated dose-dependent choice, $q\alpha/\alpha_c=(1-\epsilon)\alpha/(\alpha+\alpha_c)<1$. Subcriticality retains a positive stable equilibrium; it does not remove its lower basin boundary. Thus ray preservation alone proves no impossibility result for positive task weighting.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rank-dilution scaling]\label{prop:rank_dilution}
Assume the effective ratio scales as $\rho_\star\asymp(m_\Pi/m)^{\gamma_0}$, $\gamma_0\ge1$, and $m_\Pi\propto m^\beta$, $0\le\beta<1$. Then $\alpha_c=1/\rho_\star$ implies
\begin{equation}
\gamma \;=\; \gamma_0\,(1-\beta), \qquad \gamma_0 \ge 1,
\label{eq:rank_dilution}
\end{equation}
The separable scaling is an assumption, not a consequence of rank alone. More generally, restoration $\asymp m^a$ and contraction $\asymp m^b m_\Pi^c$ give $\gamma=a-b-c\beta$; the displayed law requires $c=a-b=\gamma_0$. A toxic norm $\asymp m_\Pi^p$ implies $c=2p$ only if contraction is proportional to its square with no other rank dependence; $\gamma_0=2p$ needs the additional equality $a-b=2p$. Neither $p$ nor $\gamma_0$ is identified by the toxic eigenvalue spectrum alone. Fixed \emph{effective} rank gives $\beta=0$; a fixed per-head output dimension need not.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Composition of rank-one PSD operators]\label{cor:additive}
Let $T=\sum_k\rho_k u_ku_k^\top$, with unit $u_k$ and $\rho_k\ge0$, in common restoration-normalized coordinates. If the directions are mutually orthogonal, $\lambda_{\max}(T)=\max_k\rho_k$; if aligned, it is $\sum_k\rho_k$. An additional orthogonal direction leaves the maximum unchanged only if its coefficient does not exceed the previous maximum.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In the orthogonal case the $u_k$ are eigenvectors; in the aligned case the sum is a single rank-one operator. These facts alone do not identify the geometry of the production model's heads.
\end{proof}

For an explicit scalar example, take $b(\sigma)=\lambda\sigma^2/(1+\sigma^2)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\pm=\frac{\lambda\pm\sqrt{\lambda^2-4(\alpha\kappa)^2}}{2\alpha\kappa},\qquad \alpha_c=\lambda/(2\kappa).
\label{eq:fp}
\end{equation}
This verifies the fold and its basin dependence. Radial auxiliary behavior,
\begin{equation}
g_a=\kappa\delta h+o(\|\delta h\|),
\label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
is an additional isotropic-curvature assumption, not a consequence of merely being stationary at a constant representation. For a general encoder its effect on variance must still be pulled back through the Jacobian in \eqref{eq:signed_variance}. In discrete training, linearizing a scalar stable equilibrium with derivative $-\ell$ yields
\begin{equation}
e_{t+1}=(1-\eta\ell)e_t,\qquad 0<\eta\ell<2,
\label{eq:parallel_contraction}
\end{equation}
where $e_t$ is a deviation from that equilibrium, not total representation scatter.

\paragraph{Power-law spectra do not determine the capacity exponent.}
For eigenvalues $\lambda_i=C i^{-(1+s)}$ with $C$ independent of rank,
$\|A_r\|_F^2=C^2\sum_{i=1}^r i^{-2(1+s)}$. Hence the Frobenius norm is bounded for $s>-1/2$, grows as $\sqrt{\log r}$ for $s=-1/2$, and as $r^{-s-1/2}$ for $s<-1/2$. A decaying spectrum ($s>-1$) therefore has power exponent at most $1/2$; for the commonly intended $s>0$ it has exponent zero. The trace instead involves $\sum_i i^{-(1+s)}$, and the operator norm is $C$ for a decreasing spectrum. Rank-dependent normalization $C(r,m)$ changes these conclusions. Thus neither a universal $\gamma_0(s)$ nor $\gamma_0=2p$ follows without specifying the norm, normalization, restoration and coupling to dynamics. More explicitly, if $\kappa\asymp m^b\|A_r\|^q$, $\|A_r\|\asymp r^p$, and $r\asymp m^\beta$, then $\gamma=a-b-qp\beta$. The restricted form $\gamma_0(1-\beta)$ requires $a-b=qp=\gamma_0$. Hence $\gamma_0=2p$ holds only under the additional quadratic coupling $q=2$ and matching restoration exponent $a-b=2p$; these are not implied by the measured width slope.

\paragraph{Generator audit.}
The original two-head generator has $W_k\in\mathbb R^{m\times r}$ with independent entries of variance $1/r$. Its combined span has rank $\min(m,2r)$ almost surely. For the averaged quadratic loss the sample-normalized Hessian is $A=2\sum_k W_kW_k^\top/(2r)$, with $\mathbb E A=2I/r$. At $r=32$, widths $32,64,128,256$ therefore have ranks $32,64,64,64$. Fixed per-head rank is not fixed combined rank over this range, and changing $r$ also changes curvature. The supplied orthonormal control instead constructs $A=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)Q^\top$ at exactly specified rank and normalization.

RS1 fixes per-head ranks $r\in\{4,8,16\}$ over widths $32,48,64,96$, so the combined ranks are exactly $8,16,32$ throughout. At anchor $3\times10^{-4}$, descriptive half-height slopes are $-0.31$, $0.45$, and $3.02$, respectively. The minimum relative scatter ranges are $0.040$--$0.068$, $0.013$--$0.018$, and $0.0056$--$0.0132$. Several curves therefore contract without reaching a near-zero floor. Using the unpenalized anchor changes the slopes to $0.65$, $5.55$, and $3.88$. These aggregate curves show strong rank and anchor dependence; they do not support one precise universal exponent. The supplied analyzer retains all twelve curves and reports the crossing and floor separately.

\paragraph{Exact-rank control.}
The orthonormal control sets the sample-normalized auxiliary Hessian to $A=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)Q^\top$ with $\operatorname{rank}A=r$ exactly, and a self-test asserts both the rank and the amplitude. Three normalizations each fix a different scalar: $\lambda=1$ (per-direction curvature fixed, so the toxic norm grows as $\sqrt r$), $\lambda=1/r$ (trace fixed) and $\lambda=1/\sqrt r$ (Frobenius fixed). The training grid tests the first two normalizations, sweeping $m\in\{32,64,128\}$ against $r\in\{4,16\}$ at seven doses and three seeds, with the loss, optimizer and data generator otherwise unchanged, gives three readings. First, at fixed width and fixed per-direction curvature the crossing is \emph{not} rank-invariant: $r=16$ sits below $r=4$ by $4.8\times$ at $m=64$ and $228\times$ at $m=32$. Second, the sign of the fitted width exponent follows the normalization rather than the rank: $r=4$ returns $-0.29$ under $\lambda=1$ and $+0.91$ under $\lambda=1/r$ on the same seeds and the same three widths. Third, the $r=4$ cells contract only to $3.5$--$11\%$ of the healthy scatter and never reach the floor, whereas $r=16$ reaches $0.4$--$1.1\%$: the rank-plateau control of the paragraph above survives exact control of the operator. Each three-width fit carries one residual degree of freedom and resolves no exponent, so the control bounds the estimator rather than the law --- with the rank and the normalization each held exactly, the fitted exponent still moves with the normalization. \texttt{controlled\_rank\_scan.py --self-test} verifies the construction and \texttt{run\_controlled\_rank\_grid.py} reproduces the twelve cells. The historical squared pairwise hinge has zero margin: tied scores can attain zero primary loss. These controls therefore test the stated synthetic objective, not whether an informative ranking objective necessarily has the same transition. The separate \texttt{--primary-loss logistic} option removes that degeneracy; no uncompleted logistic sweep is counted as evidence here.


A direct measurement on the synthetic testbed shows what the rank schedule does. The testbed uses controlled random toxic projections of rank $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ onto a low-rank primary ($2$--$8$ active directions across seeds, mean $5.3$) with a pairwise-rank loss, and sweeps a 17-point log-spaced $\alpha$ grid over $[3{\times}10^{-4},10]$ at six widths $m\in\{8,\dots,256\}$. Its toxic rank is a fixed \emph{fraction} of the width, so the testbed realizes $\beta=1$ --- the diluted end of the axis, not the undiluted anchor. Read with the relative half-height rule of Section~\ref{sec:capacity_law} it gives $\gamma=0.07$ over the six widths and $\gamma=0.01$ over the four widths used below: no capacity law at all, exactly as \eqref{eq:rank_dilution} predicts at $\beta=1$.

The exponent previously reported here, $\gamma=1.53$ ($R^2=0.91$), is the same curves read against a fixed absolute scatter level $\sigma_h=1.5\times10^{-3}$, and the artifact is mechanical. The healthy scatter itself grows with width, $\sigma_h(0)\propto m^{0.95}$ (measured on the fixed-rank family below), so a fixed absolute level is a relative depth that shallows as $m$ grows and the crossing drifts into the collapsed tail. The artifact is also directly visible: the same absolute rule applied to the rank-schedule family returns $\gamma=1.94,2.02,2.04,1.96,1.89$ for $\beta=0,\dots,1$ --- flat to within $0.15$ across the whole axis, i.e.\ blind to the parameter the law is about. The undiluted anchor is measured instead on a family whose rank does not scale with width (fixed generator rank $r$; the combined span can reach $\min(m,2r)$): $\gamma_0=2.43$ ($R^2=0.90$, four widths), in the collective branch ($\gamma_0>1$).

Both new families sweep the paper's grid extended down to $\alpha=0$ ($21$ points) at four widths $m\in\{32,64,128,256\}$, five seeds, $800$ epochs, everything else as in the testbed; the fixed-rank family is the $\beta=0$ slice of the same code path with $r$ held constant, and it agrees with the $\beta=0$ rank-schedule run in $\alpha_c$ to $+3.1\%,+1.8\%,-5.8\%,+19.7\%$ for $m=32,\dots,256$ --- two independent runs of the same schedule that differ only in seed count ($10$ against $5$), the residual being the seed scatter of the healthy anchor at large width.

Two controls on the crossing rule. First, a half-height crossing is meaningful only where the curve actually collapses: for fixed rank $r\le4$ the scatter settles on a plateau at $2$--$23\%$ of $\sigma_h(0)$ and never reaches the collapsed floor, so those cells carry no capacity threshold at all; only $r\ge8$ (plateau $\le0.9\%$) does. Second, for the synthetic families the healthy level is read at the first \emph{penalised} grid point, $\alpha=3\times10^{-4}$: at $\alpha=0$ the variance penalty is absent, so $\sigma_h(0)$ is set by the ranking loss alone, is identical for every toxic rank, and sits $30$--$40\%$ above the penalised curve's own trend. Sweeping the anchor over $\alpha\in[0,3\times10^{-3}]$ leaves the shape of $\gamma(\beta)$ unchanged --- monotone decreasing, from $\gamma_0\in[1.55,3.23]$ at $\beta=0$ to $\gamma(\beta{=}1)\in[-0.06,+0.74]$ --- so the ordering and the sign of the dilution are properties of the data, not of the crossing rule.

The rank-schedule fits below test a restricted scaling hypothesis. Substituting $m_\Pi\asymp m^\beta$ into an independently established $\alpha_c\asymp(m/m_\Pi)^{\gamma_0}$ gives $\gamma=\gamma_0(1-\beta)$; it does not establish the premise. Back-solving $\beta$ from measured $\gamma$ cannot serve as an independent measurement of toxic rank. The original generator audit further limits interpreting its per-head rank schedule as an effective-rank schedule.

The exponents above are read with the relative half-height rule, and the qualitative conclusion survives a change of rule. On HAR, taking the crossing at a fixed absolute scatter level $T\in[0.20,0.50]$ instead of the relative half-height gives $\gamma_{\mathrm{HAR}}\in[0.66,0.82]$ against $0.66$ --- a factor of $1.2$ in the fitted value, with both far below $\gamma_0$. The battery exponent is measurable only on a grid that reaches the healthy branch: earlier sweeps began at $\alpha=300$, where the relative scatter is already $0.07$--$0.11$, below the collapse threshold, so every width returned the left endpoint of the grid and the exponent was left-censored rather than fitted. Extending the grid to $\alpha\in[10^{-4},300]$ at five seeds reaches the healthy branch (relative scatter $1.000\pm0.005$ for $\alpha\le10^{-3}$) and brackets the transition at $\alpha_c=0.95,0.89,0.90,1.18$ for $m=32,\dots,256$, giving $\gamma_{\mathrm{bat}}=0.10$ ($R^2=0.43$); across crossing rules the value ranges over $[-0.10,+0.12]$: four widths resolve the width-independence of the battery transition but not its exponent.

Treating each width's crossing as one observation, the four-width OLS slopes have $95\%$ intervals $[-0.07,+4.93]$ at $\beta=0$, $[-0.16,+1.49]$ for HAR, $[-0.24,+0.43]$ for battery, and $[-1.16,+1.20]$ for RadioML: no individual exponent is separated from zero, the anchor included. The interior slopes are positive ($2.57$, CI $[0.87,4.27]$ at $\beta=0.25$; $1.86$, $[0.81,2.91]$ at $\beta=0.50$), while $\beta=1$ is consistent with zero. At the published anchor all four interior/endpoint intervals contain $\gamma_0(1-\beta)$; at $\alpha=10^{-4}$ the $\beta=0.75$ interval excludes it. These four-width estimates support dilution, not an anchor-independent coefficient.

The denser SYN8 family supplies $26$ completed $(m,\beta)$ cells, ten seeds each. Since $r(64)=32$ for every schedule, we fit $\log\alpha_c=c+\gamma_0(1-\beta)\log(m/64)$. At anchor $3\times10^{-4}$, the constrained fit gives $\gamma_0=2.50$ (nominal OLS $95\%$ CI $[1.94,3.06]$). Across anchors $\{10^{-4},3\times10^{-4}\}$ and inclusion/exclusion of $m=128$, all four conditional intervals exclude zero (lowest bound $1.04$). In the unconstrained model $c+a x+b\beta x$, $x=\log(m/64)$, every interval for $a+b$ contains zero and $-b/a=0.78$--$1.03$: the linear form is not rejected, not established. These are cell-level fit intervals, not independent-seed uncertainty: the schedules share seeds and the $m=64$ curves are identical. Joint/anchor-row point estimates range from $1.35$ to $2.87$. At $m\ge96$ the sampled curve never resolves the collapsed floor; half-height crossings interpolate the observed decline, not a verified collapse threshold. Thus SYN8 sharpens a conditional trend without supplying a precise physical exponent. \texttt{synth\_beta\_joint.py} reproduces the fits.

The four-width bound of the previous paragraph tightens when the HAR and battery sweeps are repeated at nine widths. Running the identical protocol --- same driver, seeds, epochs and $\alpha$ grids --- at $m\in\{16,24,32,48,64,96,128,192,256\}$ reproduces every cell the two grids share to floating-point equality (largest disagreement $2\times10^{-16}$), so the extended grid is the published one at finer width resolution rather than a second measurement of a different quantity. HAR's exponent becomes $\gamma_{\mathrm{HAR}}=0.38$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.10,+0.67]$, $R^2=0.60$, $n=9$): the interval narrows from the $[-0.16,+1.49]$ quoted above and, unlike it, excludes zero, so HAR is the first individual exponent the sweep separates from zero. The four-width value of the main text lies at its upper edge --- the two grids agree, but the finer one prefers the smaller exponent --- and dropping the two narrowest widths leaves the exponent at $0.60$. Battery stays where the four-width fit put it, $\gamma_{\mathrm{bat}}=-0.06$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.17,+0.06]$), consistent with width-independence and inside the rule-to-rule range quoted above. What does not tighten is the crossing itself: at $m=32$ the HAR curve reads $0.5026\pm0.0524$ at $\alpha=10^{-3}$, so the crossing sits within one seed standard error of a grid node and the interpolated $\alpha_c$ is decided by the draw as much as by the grid. Nine widths therefore sharpen the ordering and the sign of the intermediate exponent without pinning its value, and the main text keeps the four-width estimates for that reason.

Encoder architecture does not change what the probe reads. Repeating the one-probe calibration with the Transformer encoder of Section~\ref{sec:validation} at $m\in\{32,64,128\}$, ten seeds and otherwise the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:unified_protocol}, gives probe exponents $\gamma^{\mathrm{probe}}_{\mathrm{HAR}}=-0.93$ ($R^2=0.99$), $\gamma^{\mathrm{probe}}_{\mathrm{bat}}=+0.39$ ($R^2=0.87$) and $\gamma^{\mathrm{probe}}_{\mathrm{RML}}=-0.17$ ($R^2=0.79$), against $-1.63$ ($R^2=0.96$), $+0.67$ ($R^2=0.86$) and $+0.28$ ($R^2=0.40$) for the harness MLP. The signs agree on HAR (both negative) and on battery (both positive), RadioML is consistent with zero under both, and the $95\%$ intervals overlap in all three domains: the calibration's width-dependence is a property of the domain, not of the encoder. This is the capacity-side counterpart of the protection transfer of Section~\ref{sec:validation}, and \texttt{probe\_exponent.py} reproduces it from the shipped calibration runs.

These are probe exponents, not the crossing exponents of Section~\ref{sec:capacity_law}. The two estimators agree in sign on RadioML ($+0.28$ against $+0.02$) and disagree on HAR ($-1.63$ against $+0.38$) and battery ($+0.67$ against $-0.06$). The crossing locates the dose at which the \emph{trained} scatter has lost half its variance, whereas the probe reads $1/\hat\rho$ on the healthy anchor; the two place the transition within a factor of a few of each other at a fixed width (a factor of $7$ at $m{=}64$ on NYUv2) but need not share its width-scaling. Where they do not, it is the probe that leaves the admissible range: \eqref{eq:rank_dilution} admits only $\gamma\ge0$, so a negative probe exponent is not a capacity exponent. The exponents of Section~\ref{sec:capacity_law} are therefore the crossing ones, and the probe is reported for NYUv2 (Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}) because there it is the quantity \textsc{stfcal} itself gates on.

Several sweeps show bimodal per-seed scatter near an empirical crossing. This is consistent with multiple outcomes but does not uniquely identify a saddle-node bifurcation; initialization, finite training time and optimization can also generate heterogeneous outcomes.

All numbers use deterministic seeds, bf16 autocast with TF32 matmul on an NVIDIA A100 80\,GB, Adam ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$, batch 256). The relative-collapse threshold is $0.3\times$ the per-seed single-task ceiling; recovery is defined relative to the same-seed joint baseline. Code, seeds, hyper-parameters and per-fold tables ship with the repository.

\section{Generalizations and Finite-Size Theory}\label{app:general}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed attenuation in a specified model]\label{prop:attenuation}
For $T\succeq0$ in restoration-normalized coordinates, uniform attenuation $T\mapsto(1-\lambda)T$, $0\le\lambda<1$, multiplies the first-modal-contraction threshold by $1/(1-\lambda)$. At fixed dose the smallest $\lambda$ meeting $\alpha(1-\lambda)\|T\|\le1$ is $\max(0,1-1/(\alpha\|T\|))$ when $\alpha\|T\|>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All eigenvalues scale by $1-\lambda$; solve the displayed inequality. This statement concerns a fixed PSD model, not arbitrary signed or nonsymmetric filtered dynamics.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Annihilation by orthogonal compression]\label{thm:filters}
Let $T\succeq0$, $U=\mathrm{range}(T)$, and $Q=I-P_S$ be an orthogonal projector. Then $QTQ=0$ iff $U\subseteq S$. Among such deletions the unique smallest deleted subspace is $U$, and $I-P_U$ preserves its orthogonal complement. This is an algebraic statement about two-sided compression.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$QTQ=(T^{1/2}Q)^\top(T^{1/2}Q)=0$ iff $\mathrm{range}(Q)\subseteq\ker T$, equivalently $U\subseteq S$. A general one-sided backward operator $QT$ need not describe symmetric neural-network dynamics; a signed filter can even reverse contraction, so its operator norm cannot supply the missing implication.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subspace estimation bound]\label{cor:estimation}
If $\|P_U-P_{\hat U}\|\le\epsilon$ and $Q=I-P_{\hat U}$, then
\begin{equation}
\|QT\|\le\epsilon\|T\|,\qquad \|QTQ\|\le\epsilon^2\|T\|.
\label{eq:estimation}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $T=P_UTP_U$ and $\|QP_U\|\le\epsilon$ on one or both sides. Whitening must precede this argument if $T$ is restoration-normalized; an angle bound in other coordinates is insufficient. These are upper bounds on residual magnitude, not evidence of a nonzero residual or inevitable failure at a finite dose.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimum rank for a PSD compression]\label{thm:minimalrank}
Fix a PSD operator $T$ in specified Euclidean coordinates, with eigenvalues $\kappa_1\ge\cdots\ge\kappa_d\ge0$, and a threshold $\tau\ge0$. Among orthogonal projectors $Q=I-P_S$ satisfying $\|QTQ\|\le\tau$, the minimum deletion rank is
\[
r^\star=\#\{j:\kappa_j>\tau\}.
\]
Deleting $S_c=\mathrm{span}\{u_j:\kappa_j>\tau\}$ attains it. Within the class of spectral deletions, $S_c$ also minimizes any inclusion-monotone deleted energy. Uniqueness of the energy optimum requires positive energy in every additionally deleted eigendirection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The spectral choice leaves only eigenvalues at most $\tau$. For any $\dim S<r^\star$, dimension counting gives a unit vector $v\in S^\perp\cap S_c$. Its Rayleigh quotient satisfies $v^\top QTQv=v^\top Tv>\tau$, violating feasibility. For spectral deletions feasibility requires containing $S_c$, giving the energy statement by inclusion. Rotated subspaces can also attain the same minimum rank; no global minimum-energy claim over rotations follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Saturation and its limits]\label{cor:saturated}
For $\tau=1/\alpha$, the optimal rank changes in steps as $\alpha$ crosses inverse positive eigenvalues and eventually equals $\dim U$. The limiting projector is $I-P_U$, which equals full detachment only on $U$, or globally when $U$ is the entire gradient space. Exhausting a finite probe depth does not prove saturation of $U$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Which basis is justified?}
The theorem concerns a specified operator, not a mean of gradient-norm ratios. For a linear reconstruction decoder $Wh$ with squared loss, $g_a=W^\top\delta$ (up to loss normalization) and the Hessian is $W^\top W$. The uncentered second moment relevant to normalized residual energy is
\[
C_g=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{g_ag_a^\top}{(\|g_p\|+\varepsilon)^2}\right]
=W^\top\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\delta\delta^\top}{(\|g_p\|+\varepsilon)^2}\right]W.
\]
For any rank-$r$ projector $P$, the squared residual is $\operatorname{tr}((I-P)C_g)$, minimized by the top $r$ eigenvectors of $C_g$ (the Rayleigh--Ritz principle). Its square root upper-bounds the corresponding mean norm by Jensen's inequality. It does not generally minimize that mean itself. Hessian eigenvectors coincide with this basis under isotropic weighted residual covariance; representation PCA requires its leading subspace to coincide with the leading eigenspace of $C_g$. Commutation gives a common eigenbasis but does not ensure the same ordering. A nonlinear decoder additionally contributes residual-weighted second derivatives to its Hessian. Thus a nearly flat PCA residual curve is consistent with gradient energy outside leading representation components, but does not prove that the reconstruction residual is orthogonal to those components.

The variance-shrinkage rank sweep is reported in Table~\ref{tab:doserank}. For real HAR reconstruction the auxiliary and PCA bases select mean candidate ranks $22.6$ and $57.0$ at $\alpha=12$, but only four of ten seeds activate the gate. Their active ranks are $30.75$ and $62.0$, with similar aggregate accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:realbasis}); this supports basis sensitivity, not universal rank optimality. The RMS-basis pilot is retained in the released research records, including its negative accuracy result at three seeds.

\paragraph{MATR audit.}
The ten-seed cap32 run gives SOH RMSE $5.26$ for \texttt{stfcalrank} versus $5.24$ for joint; its reported rank reaches the cap in every seed. Increasing probe depth to 32 therefore does not validate minimum-rank protection. The single-seed full-depth audit gives across-example covariance traces $3.131$, $4.065$, $0.906$, and $0.821$ for joint, detachment, calibrated fallback, and the rank arm, respectively; the SOH-only reference is $1.147$. Corresponding normalized SOH prediction variances are $0.00826$, $0.01380$, $0.01253$, and $0.01019$, versus reference $0.01379$. Representation variance, effective rank and prediction variance must be reported separately: these values neither establish representation recovery nor a constant-prediction collapse. Historical battery checkpoints were selected on their evaluation split; they remain exploratory. A new cell-disjoint validation protocol fits preprocessing on training cells and evaluates test data only after checkpoint selection.

\paragraph{Matched forward versus backward filtering.}
For fixed $U$, define $\widetilde H_f=H-C_BHUU^\top$ and $\widetilde H_b=\operatorname{sg}(H)+D-C_BDUU^\top$, where $D=H-\operatorname{sg}(H)$ and $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients. Both maps have Jacobian $G\mapsto G-C_BGUU^\top$, but only the second preserves the auxiliary forward value. The same Jacobian therefore need not yield the same update: the loss gradient is evaluated at a different input. Even at full rank, the batch-mean gradient channel remains, unlike full detachment.

We isolate this distinction on HAR reconstruction at $\alpha=100$, $m=R=64$, with identical warm-up, seed, calibrated basis and selected rank within each pair. All ten seeds open the gate. Forward filtering gives primary accuracy $0.9507\pm0.0033$, reconstruction MSE $0.05112\pm0.01083$, and relative scatter $0.681\pm0.039$; backward-only filtering gives $0.9478\pm0.0058$, $0.02053\pm0.00115$, and $0.597\pm0.061$. Both auxiliary metrics are evaluated on unfiltered representations. The paired accuracy difference (backward minus forward) is $-0.00285$ (95\% $t$ interval $[-0.00815,0.00245]$); reconstruction improves in every seed, but relative scatter decreases. Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p$ values over the three outcomes are $0.242$, $0.00586$ and $0.0195$. This exploratory comparison shows an auxiliary-fit versus scatter tradeoff, not a superior primary-task algorithm. Joint and detachment accuracy are $0.9461$ and $0.9431$ in the same harness. A three-seed $\alpha=12$ pilot, including its two closed-gate pairs, is also retained. The scripts \texttt{filter\_semantics\_audit.py} and \texttt{summarize\_filter\_semantics.py} supply the semantic checks, raw records and paired summary. All probe algebra runs in fp32, including the eigendecomposition outside the gradient-extraction block; a regression check verifies invariance to enclosing bf16 autocast.

\paragraph{Parameter-space variance constraint.}
A complementary experimental intervention uses $v=\nabla_\theta V$, proposed update $u_0=G_p+\alpha G_a$, and slack $h=V-V_{\min}$. For $k>0$ and $v\ne0$, the closest update satisfying $v^\top u\le kh$ is
\[
u^\star=u_0-\frac{(v^\top u_0-kh)_+}{\|v\|^2}v.
\]
The KKT conditions prove this Euclidean halfspace projection. Under a well-defined continuous flow, $\dot h\ge-kh$ preserves feasibility on this \emph{fixed batch}. At $v=0$ feasibility requires $kh\ge0$. Discrete updates require checking the actual post-step variance: the supplied SGD implementation backtracks and restores parameters on failure. The argument covers neither unseen data nor Adam without modeling its applied update. This applies established constrained-gradient ideas~\citep{allibhoy2024safe}, without claiming halfspace projection as novel; empirical benefits remain to be established.

\paragraph{Matched attenuation.}
The fixed-dose model motivates comparing deletion with positive attenuation on the same harness (UCI-HAR, $m=64$, sixty epochs). Table~\ref{tab:attenuation} reports the exploratory grid. The calibrated multiplier uses the unprojected mean gradient ratio, rather than the radial diagnostic in \eqref{eq:calrule}; hence $c=1$ is not an identified dynamical threshold. Values $c=0.1$ perform competitively with the tested fixed multipliers, supporting scalar attenuation as a necessary baseline. They do not establish a universal optimal fraction of a critical dose: the safety constant was compared using these outcomes, sample sizes are small, and non-finite runs occur. At $\alpha=300$, seed $0$ diverges for every arm, including detachment, because auxiliary heads can still train during detached warm-up; numerical failure cannot be equated with representation collapse.

\begin{proposition}[Sampling fluctuation of a frozen statistic]\label{prop:finitebatch}
For independent identically distributed scalar diagnostics $X_i$ with finite variance $\sigma_X^2$, $\hat\rho=B^{-1}\sum_iX_i$ has variance $\sigma_X^2/B$ and obeys a central limit theorem. Under a normal approximation the probability that the \emph{gate} $\alpha\hat\rho>1$ opens is $\Phi(\sqrt B(\rho-1/\alpha)/\sigma_X)$. This is not the probability of training collapse. Batch-dependent centering or fitted bases require additional analysis or an independent frozen calibration sample.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use variance additivity and the central limit theorem. For a ratio $A/P$, the delta-method variance includes $-2\mu_A\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)/\mu_P^3$ as well as the two marginal variance terms. Mean per-example ratios and ratios of means are distinct estimands.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional calibration bound]\label{prop:calibration}
Assume independent $X_i\in[0,M]$ with common mean $\rho$ and population variance at most $v_0$. Bernstein's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb P(|\hat\rho-\rho|\ge\varepsilon)\le2\exp\left[-\frac{B\varepsilon^2}{2v_0+2M\varepsilon/3}\right].
\label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
Thus an error probability at most $\delta$ follows if
\begin{equation}
B\ge(2v_0+2M\varepsilon/3)\varepsilon^{-2}\log(2/\delta).
\label{eq:samplesize}
\end{equation}
A nonzero primary-gradient denominator alone does not provide a uniform bound $M$. Population bounds cannot be replaced by observed sample moments without a separate valid empirical-concentration result.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the bounded-variable Bernstein inequality to the centered independent sum and solve for $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Certifying a frozen gate or nested rank rule]\label{cor:certificate}
Under Proposition~\ref{prop:calibration}, a gate with population margin $d=|\rho-1/\alpha|>0$ has error probability at most $2\exp[-Bd^2/(2v_0+2Md/3)]$. For $R+1$ fixed residual statistics with uniformly positive margins, a union bound controls all their threshold decisions and hence the selected nested rank. This does not identify the statistics with Hessian eigenvalues, certify an adaptively fitted basis without conditioning, or certify a subsequent training trajectory.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A wrong threshold decision requires an estimation error at least its margin; apply the bound and then the union bound. Strict margins exclude equality cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Local linear noise floor]\label{prop:lyapunov}
For deviations $e_{t+1}=a e_t+\eta\xi_t$ from a fixed equilibrium, assume $|a|<1$ and independent mean-zero innovations of variance $D$. The stationary variance is $\eta^2D/(1-a^2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The second-moment recursion is $\mathbb E e_{t+1}^2=a^2\mathbb E e_t^2+\eta^2D$; sum the convergent geometric series. This is variance of deviations, not total representation scatter. Divergence of a local linear approximation as $|a|\to1$ does not prove a universal ordering of half-height and binary-collapse thresholds in a nonlinear experiment.
\end{proof}

Repeated frozen-anchor probes provide an empirical sampling check. On HAR, $2000$ resampled batches per size and five seeds yield fitted slopes $-0.500$ for the coefficient of variation and $-0.494$ for the standard deviation, while the mean is approximately flat (slope $+0.006$). Table~\ref{tab:certificate} retains the numerical plug-in envelopes computed in those runs. Since its variance and range were estimated from the same data, and the implemented batch statistic can share a center across examples, these envelopes are diagnostic comparisons rather than rigorous population certificates. \subsection{Prediction variance normalization and target $z$-scoring}\label{app:zscore}

A linear prediction head $\hat y=w^\top h+b$ introduces a scale ambiguity: shrinking centered representations by $1/c$ and expanding $w$ by $c$ preserves predictions. Shrinking both generally does not preserve the regression loss, nor can constant predictions fit varying targets exactly. Consequently prediction variance and across-example representation covariance should both accompany task error. Prediction normalization and target scaling are practical controls, not proofs of a universal prediction-level trap.

The historical regression recipe standardizes targets and divides predictions by a detached batch standard deviation. This changes optimization and can reduce sensitivity to scale, but detached normalization does not implement the derivative of a truly scale-invariant loss. Softmax also does not normalize logit scale: changing that scale changes probabilities. These operations therefore require empirical ablations, with scaling statistics fit only on training data.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{STF training protocol (per domain, per configuration)}
\label{alg:stf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} domain data $\mathcal{D}$, encoder width $m$, toxicity schedule $\{\alpha\}$, seeds
\STATE \textbf{Phase 1 -- health anchor:} train the single-task encoder ($\alpha=0$), record healthy scatter $\mathcal{S}_{single}=\mathcal{S}_h(\theta^*)$ and healthy spectral ratio $\rho_0$.
\STATE \textbf{Phase 2 -- calibration:} sweep $\alpha$ with mode \textsc{joint}; record $\rho$, relative scatter $\mathcal{S}_h/\mathcal{S}_{single}$, collapse flag; locate $\alpha_c$ by the threshold crossing.
\STATE \textbf{Phase 3 -- repair:} at a selected dose, train each arm on $L_p(H)+\alpha L_a(\widetilde H)$, using its specified forward map $\widetilde H$ (or detached $H$ for \textsc{stf-0}); backpropagate this loss and monitor the gradient diagnostic and relative scatter.
\STATE \textbf{Report:} collapse rate, recovery rate, $\rho$, $\mathcal{S}_h/\mathcal{S}_{single}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Released calibrated forward filters: radial \textsc{stfcal} and capped \textsc{stfcalrank}}
\label{alg:stfcal}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} data $\mathcal{D}$, encoder width $m$, operating weight $\alpha$, probe depth $R$, warm-up length $W$
\STATE \textbf{Warm-up:} train $\theta$ with the auxiliary branch detached ($\nabla_\theta L_a\equiv0$) for $W$ epochs; record the healthy anchor $\mathcal{S}^\star$ of \eqref{eq:cross_sample_scatter}
\STATE \textbf{Probe:} obtain per-example gradients on an unfiltered frozen batch, radial projectors $\Pi_{c,i}$, and principal basis $U$. With $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$, compute $\hat\rho=B^{-1}\sum_i\|\Pi_{c,i}g_a^{(i)}\|/(\|g_p^{(i)}\|+\varepsilon)$ and $\hat\rho_{\mathrm{res}}[r]=B^{-1}\sum_i\|(I-U_rU_r^\top)g_a^{(i)}\|/(\|g_p^{(i)}\|+\varepsilon)$ for $r=0,\ldots,R$.
\STATE \textbf{Calibrate:} $g\leftarrow\mathbf1[\alpha\hat\rho\ge1]$; $\mathcal R\leftarrow\{r\le R:\alpha\hat\rho_{\mathrm{res}}[r]<1\}$; choose $r\leftarrow\max(1,\min\mathcal R)$ if nonempty, else $r\leftarrow R$. Record whether a feasible rank exists.
\STATE \textbf{Latch:} freeze $g,r,U$. If $g=0$, pass $H$ to the auxiliary head. Otherwise, \textsc{stfcalrank} passes $H-C_BHU_rU_r^\top$; \textsc{stfcal} passes $h_i-\langle h_i-\bar h,u_i\rangle u_i$, $u_i=(h_i-\bar h)/\max(\|h_i-\bar h\|,\varepsilon)$. Differentiate the resulting auxiliary loss through this map.
\STATE \textbf{Report:} gate, $r$, $\hat\rho$, $\mathcal{S}_h/\mathcal{S}^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The repository supplies the scripts and recorded results listed in its reproduction manifest; data-dependent reruns additionally require the stated datasets and preprocessing. New audits are identified separately from historical experiments.
The cross-domain protocol (\texttt{cross\_domain\_mtl.py}), the flagship battery application (\texttt{battery\_bmtl\_v3.py}), and the NYUv2 dense benchmark (\texttt{nyu\_bmtl.py}) implement the whole STF family, including the calibrated variants \textsc{stfcal} and \textsc{stfcalrank}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:stfcal} specifies the unified and real-head forward maps, with application-specific details in each driver; the reported conflict-resolution baselines (PCGrad, CAGrad, FAMO) share the same harness, and so does Table~\ref{tab:compose}. The remaining families (MGDA, Aligned-MTL, Nash-MTL, IMTL-G, IMTL-L, GradNorm, uncertainty weighting) are benchmarked through it: \texttt{moo\_combiners.py} ports the kernels of the reference releases of the original authors and \texttt{cross\_domain\_mtl.py} combines them in one code path, so the collapse rates of Table~\ref{tab:conflict} and the battery numbers of Table~\ref{tab:conflict_num} are measured under the same optimizer, schedule and batch as the baselines they extend, with each family at its published defaults.

Each experiment is specified as a JSON job manifest --- one entry per (domain, width, mode, seed) cell, carrying the exact command line, output path and log path --- and executed by a deterministic runner that skips cells whose outputs already exist; the manifests cover the four-domain collapse scans, the width-resolved capacity sweeps, the conflict-composition grid, the real-auxiliary-head comparisons, the auxiliary-weight sweep of Table~\ref{tab:aux_sweep}, the calibrated validation of Section~\ref{sec:stfcal}, the NYUv2 calibration, dose--response, and dense benchmark of Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}, and the multi-task-baseline coverage of Table~\ref{tab:conflict} in three domains.
Every reported cell uses fixed integer seeds ($4$--$10$ per cell), each table states its seed count, and the JSON outputs retain per-seed values.

The original unified runs use one NVIDIA A100 80\,GB and Adam at $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$; bf16 autocast with TF32 matmul; the calibration probe is evaluated in fp32 outside autocast on a detached clone. The cost of the filter relative to the alternatives is measured directly. Two guards keep the ratio honest. The run starts only after the device reports idle for three consecutive samples, and the row order is rotated by one position per repetition, so no method is always measured first or last. Both matter: the same script, run while a co-tenant held the device, reported the reference epoch at $0.180$\,s instead of $0.099$\,s and FAMO at $0.0\%$ instead of $+31\%$, because the row that is always measured last absorbs whatever drift the device has. \section{NYUv2: Dense Perception Beyond Time Series}\label{app:nyu}

NYUv2~\citep{silberman2012indoor,ladicky2014pulling} provides 795 training and 654 test scenes with per-pixel semantic segmentation (13 valid classes; boundary pixels labeled $-1$ and masked in loss and metric), monocular depth, and surface normals; we downsample to $144\times192$. The benchmark encoder is convolutional (four stride-2 blocks with batch-norm--ReLU, $3\to32\to64\to128\to256$ channels, flattened to $d{=}128$) with linear heads for the three tasks; losses are cross-entropy (ignore index $-1$), $L_1$ depth, and a cosine normal loss with zero-norm pixels masked; metrics are pixel accuracy, mIoU, depth RMSE/MAE, and mean angular error, all read at the final epoch over ten seeds with no test-set selection. Two auxiliary branches are studied: \emph{proj}, the controlled variance-shrinkage toxifier of the unified protocol over a fixed random projection to $d/4$, at the calibrated supercritical dose $\alpha{=}1$; and \emph{ae}, a trainable three-layer head reconstructing the input --- the production-style reconstruction aux of the battery flagship --- at $\alpha{=}250$, $3.4\times$ its calibrated $\alpha_c$. In the unified-protocol variant the primary task is the $9\times12$ coarse depth grid (108 outputs) and the harness MLP, optimizer, probe, and collapse criterion are exactly those of Section~\ref{sec:unified_protocol}.

One probe per seed after four detached warm-up epochs on the harness encoder gives $\hat\rho=2.26\pm0.16$, $2.01\pm0.10$, $1.64\pm0.09$, $1.29\pm0.05$ at $m{=}32,64,128,256$ (per-seed ranges $2.06$--$2.65$, $1.90$--$2.20$, $1.51$--$1.77$, $1.22$--$1.38$), i.e.\ $\alpha_c=1/\hat\rho=0.44/0.50/0.61/0.78$: the critical dose \emph{grows} with width, and the four-point log--log fit gives capacity exponent $\gamma=0.27$ ($R^2=0.98$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.15,+0.40]$), inside the HAR--RadioML range and consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_dilution}. The estimator differs from the one used for the other domains, and the difference is not cosmetic: here $\alpha_c$ comes from the one-probe calibration ($1/\hat\rho$) at four widths, whereas the HAR, battery, and RadioML exponents are read off the scatter half-height crossing. That crossing, applied here, returns the \emph{opposite} ordering --- $\gamma=-0.77$ over the same four widths --- and the inversion is not an artefact of the threshold that reads it: sweeping the crossing level from $0.3$ to $0.7$ moves the exponent only between $-0.94$ and $-0.73$ ($R^2\ge0.99$), whereas the same sweep on HAR stays positive throughout ($+0.34$ to $+0.46$). The two estimators are therefore not interchangeable on this domain: they place the transition within a factor of $7$ of each other at $m{=}64$ ($0.069$ against $0.50$) but disagree on how it moves with width. We report the probe because it is the quantity \textsc{stfcal} gates on, and quote the crossing's value beside it so the two can be compared directly. The crossing is not a grid artefact --- extending the sweep four decades lower puts the scatter back on the anchor ($0.99$--$1.01$ at $\alpha=10^{-6}$, against $0.64$--$0.83$ at $\alpha=0.01$, the shallowest dose previously swept) and leaves the crossing where it was --- so the inversion is a property of the scatter curve, not of where the grid starts. $\gamma_{\mathrm{NYU}}=0.27$ is therefore the probe-based value and is not directly comparable with the crossing-based exponents of Section~\ref{sec:capacity_law}. The remaining degree of freedom is \emph{when} the probe is read; Appendix~\ref{app:warmup} measures it and finds the probe exponent stable in the read time here (it ranges over $0.17$, against $2.55$ on HAR, which changes sign), and that stability is what makes the probe usable as the estimator on this domain. On the benchmark encoder the same probe reads $\hat\rho=3.27\pm0.54$ (per-seed $2.45$--$3.92$), predicting $\alpha_c\approx0.31$; the \emph{ae} head reads $\hat\rho=0.0136\pm0.0015$, predicting $\alpha_c\approx73$.

Table~\ref{tab:nyu_dose}: joint collapses $10/10$ for every $\alpha\ge0.5$, while \textsc{stfcal} achieves $0/10$ only for $\alpha\ge1$, with the gate open in every seed (rank 3) and relative scatter $1.00$; at $\alpha{=}0.5$, exactly the predicted shell ($1/\alpha=2.0$ against per-seed $\hat\rho\in[1.90,2.20]$), the gate opens in exactly the five seeds with $\hat\rho>2$ and every gate-open seed is protected (rank 1 suffices) --- an empirical threshold comparison, subject to the scope restrictions of Corollary~\ref{cor:certificate}. At sub-critical doses \textsc{stfcal} is indistinguishable from joint. The collapse is metric-silent: joint depth RMSE \emph{improves} monotonically from $1.335$ to $1.092$ ($-18\%$) as the relative scatter falls from $0.62$ to $0.04$, so the supercritical joint configuration \emph{beats} every repaired one on the primary metric while its representation has collapsed to $4\%$ of healthy scatter --- the operational case for monitoring $\rho$ rather than the loss (cf.\ the MATR case in Section~\ref{sec:validation}). Wilcoxon on per-seed relative scatter (joint vs.\ \textsc{stfcal}) gives $p=2\times10^{-3}$ at every supercritical $\alpha$, the smallest two-sided value attainable at $n{=}10$.

Table~\ref{tab:real_aux} reports the real-auxiliary comparison at $m=64$ and ten seeds. The two reconstruction heads are benign (no collapse; filtering them is harmless-to-mildly-harmful, as the near-zero measured $\rho$ predicts). The training-subject auxiliary classifier exposes a task tradeoff: joint training predicts training identity (probe accuracy $0.646$ against $1/21$ chance) at a primary-task cost, and filtering improves the primary task while reducing this classifier accuracy to $0.055$. This training-head measurement is not a held-out privacy attack. For the SNR head at the tested high dose, joint collapses in every seed, \textsc{stf-hard} and \textsc{stf-soft} cannot recover it, and full deletion performs better in the tested comparison. Measured $\rho$ separates toxic from benign heads by orders of magnitude in every domain --- the deployment-time decision the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis} calls for.

The experiments delimit where the diagnostics and filters work. Some task metrics improve despite reduced scatter, some real heads are benign at the tested dose, and RadioML has false negatives. A latched warm-up basis can drift, but the subspace-error bound alone does not identify that drift as the cause of an observed failure. Resampling a frozen probe examines sampling variation; it does not certify future training states. Numerical instability and loss of plasticity~\citep{dohare2024loss} are further possible failure mechanisms, outside the fixed-operator analysis.

A shared temporal encoder maps each cell's per-cycle window $x\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$ (normalized voltage, current, temperature, resistance) to a representation $h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$; the encoder stacks a \textsc{TSMixer}~\citep{ekambaram2023tsmixer} feature/temporal-mixing block and a gated state-space block~\citep{gu2022s4,gu2023mamba} for long-range degradation context. Three heads share $h$: a SOH regression head ($L_2$), a RUL regression head ($L_1$), and the auxiliary reconstruction/distributional heads of Definition~\ref{def:degmin}.

RUL targets are right-censored, which governs every number below. A cell that has not crossed its end-of-life threshold when recording stops carries only a \emph{lower bound} on its remaining life, so writing the observation depth in as an exact target injects the contradiction ``$\text{RUL}=0$ while $\text{SOH}\approx90\%$'' and teaches the model to predict zero life for healthy cells. The problem is severe: $59.2\%$ of MATR samples ($96/185$ cells) never reach EOL within their window, and MATR summary arrays open with an initialization zero, so a naive threshold test latches onto index $0$ for a further $48/185$ cells and assigns $\text{RUL}=0$ to \emph{their entire lifetime}. Discarding them would leave a $14$-cell test split, so we supervise the censored cells with the classical Tobit-style form~\citep{tobin1958estimation}: $|\hat r-r|$ for an exact observation and the one-sided hinge $\max(0,r_{\text{lb}}-\hat r)$ for a censored one, penalising only predictions of \emph{less} remaining life than demonstrated. Keeping the EOL search within the physically valid suffix of each trajectory recovers the $48$ zero-prefixed cells rather than dropping them (Section~\ref{sec:bmtl_ablation}).

Battery degradation is non-stationary: fast-charging and extreme-temperature windows carry disproportionate failure information yet are underrepresented. Following the multiplicative-gate principle (product beats sum under low signal-to-noise), BatteryMTL up-weights the critical windows by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w_i = &\;w_i^{(\mathrm{rate})}\;\cdot\;w_i^{(\mathrm{temp})}\;\cdot\;w_i^{(\mathrm{degrad})},\\
w_i^{(\mathrm{rate})} = &\;1+\omega\,\mathbf{1}[\text{C-rate}_i>r_q],\\
w_i^{(\mathrm{temp})} = &\;1+\omega\,\mathbf{1}[T_i<t_{lo}\vee T_i>t_{hi}],\\
w_i^{(\mathrm{degrad})} = &\;1+\omega\,\tfrac{|\Delta\text{SOH}_i|}{\max_j|\Delta\text{SOH}_j|},\\
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:triweight}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:triweight} is a soft AND over rate, temperature, and degradation rate. Its thresholds are \emph{dataset-calibrated}: $r_q$ is the $70$th percentile of the training-fold C-rate and $t_{lo},t_{hi}$ its $10$th and $90$th temperature percentiles. Fixed physical cuts cannot serve here. MATR is a high-throughput fast-charge campaign in which $99.8\%$ of cycles exceed $1.5$C, so a $1.5$C cut marks almost every sample critical and erases the contrast the gate exists to create, and its $31$--$36^\circ$C band never crosses a $0/40^\circ$C rule. Percentile thresholds instead preserve \emph{which} windows count as hardest. Per-condition evaluation quantifies the gain on the hardest regimes (Section~\ref{sec:bmtl_ablation}), and the SOH-only / RUL-only models set the ceiling the joint model should exceed.

\subsection{Relocated measurements}

The two results below are stated in condensed form in Sections~\ref{sec:bmtl} and~\ref{sec:conflict_ablations}; the full measurements are given here.

Composing a filter with a conflict operator at supercritical $\alpha$ (Table~\ref{tab:compose}) confirms the ordering. (i)~Because toxic gradients are aligned, a conflict operator on the \emph{unfiltered} branch has no opposed pair to act on: on battery it leaves the encoder collapsed in every seed (collapse rate $1.0$, relative scatter $0.029$/$0.030$ against the single-task $1.0$) even as the scalar metric moves --- the metric--representation decoupling again. (ii)~The filter alone (\textsc{stf-hard}$+$sum) drives the collapse rate to $0.0$, restores the scatter to $1.00$, and recovers SOH-RMSE from $0.611$ to $0.294$, essentially the single-task ceiling; layering conflict resolution \emph{after} it moves the metric by less than seed noise ($0.283$/$0.291$) and leaves the scatter on the healthy branch ($1.00$--$1.18$). HAR and RadioML reproduce the ordering: the two interventions complement each other, but only when \emph{sequenced}.

Battery degradation is non-stationary, so BatteryMTL multiplies three \emph{dataset-calibrated} gates over C-rate, temperature, and degradation rate, a soft AND with percentile thresholds rather than fixed physical cuts. It leaves aggregate SOH unchanged but improves the hardest windows: a single-term attribution isolates the fast-charge gain to the rate factor ($0.95$ SOH-RMSE without it) and the extreme-temperature gain to the temperature factor ($2.35$ without it). The aggregate rows are in Table~\ref{tab:bmtl} (Appendix~\ref{app:moved}).

\subsection{Why the Probe Cannot Be Tracked Online}\label{app:online}

Section~\ref{sec:stfcal} rejects an online $\rho$-gate, and the reason is measurable. We re-ran the unfiltered joint path with the monitor on (\texttt{--rho-trace}), five seeds per cell, over the full schedule ($1730$ steps on HAR, $350$ on battery). The monitor only records: the aggregate rows of these runs reproduce the \textsc{bscale} cells they overlap exactly ($28$ of $28$ matched cells, $\max|\Delta|=0$ over the metric, the collapse rate, the relative scatter and $\hat\rho$), so the trajectories are not a perturbed training run.

Three facts follow. First, the probe starts at the one-batch floor and \emph{rises}: on HAR it runs from $0.017$ to $1.9\times10^5$ at $m{=}32$, from $0.010$ to $1.0\times10^5$ at $m{=}64$, and from $0.006$ to $3.5\times10^4$ at $m{=}256$. Second, the rise is not a toxicity signal. With the toxifier \emph{inert} ($\alpha{=}0$) it reaches the same magnitudes --- $1.9\times10^5$, $1.0\times10^5$ and $3.5\times10^4$ --- and the lowest dose swept ($\alpha{=}10^{-4}$) lands in the same range, so whatever drives the rise, it is not the auxiliary weight. The only dose-dependence is downward: at $m{=}32$ the endpoint falls from $1.9\times10^5$ at $\alpha{=}0$ to $5.3\times10^2$ at $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ and $1.1\times10^2$ at $\alpha{=}3\times10^{-2}$. Third, because the probe rises, the gate $\alpha\hat\rho\ge1$ opens at \emph{every} dose eventually: in all five seeds of ten HAR cells whose endpoint scatter is at or above the crossing (from $0.50$ to $1.04$) it opens between steps $110$ and $590$ of $1730$. An online gate built on the running probe would therefore delete representation directions on runs that never collapse.

Battery is the contrast that motivates calibrating per domain rather than tracking: there the probe stays $O(1)$ ($0.29$--$0.49$ at step $0$, $0.3$--$2.9$ at the end), and the gate opens in one of the thirty-five seeds whose scatter stays above the crossing, at the final step. Whether a fixed online rule is inert or spurious is set by the domain's primary-gradient scale, which is what the healthy-branch calibration measures once. \texttt{repro/scripts/rho\_trace\_analyze.py} prints both tables from the shipped runs.

\subsection{What the Calibration Commits To: the Read Time Sets the Gate's Floor}\label{app:warmup}

The calibration reads $\hat\rho$ once, on a detached healthy anchor, and both the gate and the rank follow from that single read; every shipped run reads it after four primary-only epochs. To see what that choice is worth we re-ran the identical calibration with \emph{only} the read time $W$ varied over $\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ --- HAR and battery at five widths $32$--$512$ and ten seeds, NYUv2 at four widths and ten seeds. The $W{=}4$ column is the shipped configuration and reproduces it exactly: $\max|\Delta|=0$ across all $100$ HAR and battery cells, and $\hat\rho=2.258/2.008/1.637/1.285$ on NYUv2 against the $2.26/2.01/1.64/1.29$ of Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}. The sweep therefore varies nothing but the read time.

The read is a trajectory quantity, and how strongly it moves is a property of the domain. On HAR the probe at $m{=}32$ climbs from $0.63$ to $6.9\times10^4$ across the sweep --- five decades --- and at $m{=}512$ from $51.9$ to $1.6\times10^4$; on battery it stays $O(1)$ throughout ($0.96$ to $1.92$ at $m{=}32$, $0.058$ to $0.67$ at $m{=}512$); on NYUv2 it moves by a factor of $2.2$ ($1.94$ to $4.19$ at $m{=}32$). The exponent $\alpha_c=1/\hat\rho$ inherits the drift: read as a width-scaling, the probe exponent runs from $-2.00$ to $+0.55$ on HAR --- it changes sign --- from $+0.37$ to $+0.97$ on battery, and from $+0.11$ to $+0.27$ on NYUv2. This is the measured reason the paper reads the capacity law off the crossing for HAR, battery and RadioML and off the probe for NYUv2 (Section~\ref{sec:capacity_law}, Appendix~\ref{app:nyu}): a probe exponent is a property of the domain only where $\hat\rho$ is stable in the read time, and where it is not, the probe's width-scaling is dominated by how fast each width's primary gradient decays --- the denominator of $\hat\rho$ --- rather than by the encoder's capacity.

The same mechanism fixes what the gate can see. The gate opens on $\alpha\hat\rho\ge1$, so the read time sets its floor $1/\hat\rho$: on HAR at $m{=}64$ the floor is $1.34$ at $W{=}1$, $0.181$ at $W{=}2$, $9.07\times10^{-3}$ at $W{=}4$ and $1.7\times10^{-5}$ at $W{=}32$, against a measured transition at $\alpha_c\in[0.001,0.01]$; only $W\ge4$ puts the floor at or below the dose the filter is meant to defend. Measured on the shipped HAR repair recipe ($m{=}64$, ten seeds, sixty epochs, \textsc{joint}/\textsc{stf-0}/\textsc{stfcal} in the same run) at a marginal dose $\alpha=0.03$, three times the transition: at $W{=}1$ and $W{=}2$ the gate stays shut in all ten seeds and \textsc{stfcal} collapses in $10/10$, with metric $0.947$ and relative scatter $0.19$ and $0.20$ --- the unfiltered joint numbers ($0.949$, $0.19$) to within seed noise --- whereas from $W{=}4$ on the gate opens in every seed, \textsc{stfcal} collapses in $0/10$ and the relative scatter returns to $0.99$--$1.00$. At a dose far above any floor ($\alpha=100$) the gate opens for every read and the repair is independent of the read time ($0/10$ collapsed at all six). A short warm-up leaves the gate closed in these runs and fails to prevent the measured scatter loss. The resulting trajectory includes detached warm-up and need not equal joint training from initialization. The operating condition is checkable before deployment --- record whether the chosen $W$ makes $1/\hat\rho$ lie below the operating dose; this activation check alone does not validate protection --- and at four epochs it is satisfied, the floor landing on the measured transition. \texttt{repro/scripts/warmup\_sensitivity.py} prints both tables from the shipped runs.

\section{Anti-Collapse Regularizers at Matched Doses}\label{app:reg}

Table~\ref{tab:reg} runs the variance/covariance family of Table~\ref{tab:related} at each domain's Table~\ref{tab:conflict} dose and seeds, adding the penalty to the plain joint path with weight $\lambda$ on the shared representation: VICReg's variance hinge and off-diagonal covariance term, and Barlow Twins' redundancy reduction. VICReg's invariance term is dropped, there being no augmented view to be invariant to. Every row is compared against the same \textsc{joint} run, so the baseline is the unfiltered path and not a re-tuned primary objective.

Neither family restores the representation to the single-task anchor, and the weight needed to reach the criterion is a property of the domain, not of the penalty: VICReg needs $\lambda{=}10$ on HAR, $\lambda{=}300$ on battery and $\lambda{=}10000$ on RadioML --- three orders of magnitude apart, with the collapse rate still $1.0$ at $\lambda{=}1$, $100$ and $1000$ respectively --- and Barlow Twins never clears it in any of the three. Above that weight the representation settles where the penalty puts it. On HAR the variance hinge saturates: from $\lambda{=}10$ to $10^4$ the relative scatter is flat at $0.36$ while accuracy falls from $0.944$ to $0.821$, and the accuracy at the clearing weight is indistinguishable from both \textsc{joint} ($0.945$) and \textsc{stf-0} --- the HAR metric is collapse-insensitive, as Table~\ref{tab:unified_repair} reports. On battery the scatter is monotone in $\lambda$ and crosses the anchor, and the primary metric has an interior minimum near the crossing: $0.339$ at $\lambda{=}300$ (relative scatter $0.59$) against the $0.297$ that \textsc{stf-0} reaches at the anchor, degrading on both sides ($0.390$ at $10^3$, $0.423$ at $10^4$). \textsc{stf-0} is better on 9 of the 10 seeds. RadioML separates the criterion from the metric outright: at $\lambda{=}10000$ the collapse rate does reach $0.0$, but the relative scatter has overshot the anchor to $1.29$ and accuracy is $0.215$ --- below the $0.256$ of the \emph{collapsed} \textsc{joint} path and far below the $0.341$ that \textsc{stf-0} reaches at the anchor, so the penalty has bought a healthy scatter at the cost of the task it was added to help. Barlow Twins drives the absolute scatter to $0.011$ on HAR, $0.012$ on battery and $0.013$ on RadioML, $172$, $6$ and $24$ times below the respective anchors: it clears the criterion nowhere, and on HAR it takes accuracy down to $0.34$--$0.44$, on RadioML to $0.11$.

A finer grid around each crossing ($\lambda\in\{2,5,20,50,200,500\}$ on HAR and $\lambda\in\{200,500,700,1500,2000,5000\}$ on battery, ten seeds) narrows both readings without changing them: the battery threshold sits between $150$ and $200$, and the lowest battery metric anywhere on the grid is $0.339$ at $\lambda{=}300$ (relative scatter $0.59$), still above \textsc{stf-0}; the HAR scatter is flat at $0.36$ for every weight above the crossing. The RadioML crossing was not refined, so its threshold is only located between $\lambda{=}1000$, where the collapse rate is still $1.0$, and $\lambda{=}10000$, where it reaches $0.0$.

The collapse criterion is a threshold on relative scatter. A penalty with a global weight has a fixed point of its own, set by $\lambda$ rather than by the data, and the single-task anchor is not where it lies.
